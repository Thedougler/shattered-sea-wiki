# Hunter Shark

Hunter sharks are the working shark of the Scatter — large enough to kill, common enough that experienced sailors stop flinching and start paying attention instead. They follow the things ships leave behind: bilge discharge, fish-cleaning waste, blood from cargo loading, and the general chum trail any working harbour generates without meaning to. Where there are boats working water regularly, hunter sharks learn the schedule.

The [[Central-Strait|Central Strait]] has them year-round in the deeper lanes, trailing merchant convoys and patrol routes alike. The [[content/shattered-sea/places/crown-islands/index|Crown Islands]] harbour approaches see more incidents than any port authority records cleanly — most involve swimmers rather than vessels, and most are documented as accidents rather than attacks, because the distinction matters to the insurance. At [[Kalowe|Kalowe]], the reef gap that opens the lagoon channels enough current to hold baitfish, which holds hunter sharks, which the dry-dock workers have quietly learned to account for. A caulker who fell from a staging barge at the reef mouth came back aboard with a bite across the thigh and a strong opinion about night shifts. The gap now has a rope with a bell on it. ^[inferred]
