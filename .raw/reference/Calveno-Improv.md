# Calveno — Improv Reference

Quick-build tools for names, locations, and NPCs that fit the city. Use when the party goes somewhere you didn't prep.

---

## Linguistic Roots

| Culture | Root | Feel |
|---|---|---|
| Calveno human | Northern Italian / Venetian | Melodic, vowel-ending, names that sound like a trade city evolved them over centuries |
| Tessarine | Elevated Italian / Genoese noble | Longer, more formal, surnames that imply old money and lineage |
| Rattkin | Short Anglo + descriptor surname | One syllable or two; surname is a visible physical trait |
| Halfling (local) | Absorbed Italian first names, Anglo surnames | First names match the city; surnames stayed from wherever they came from |

## Location Naming

Calveno places are named from observable facts, not mythology. When you need a name for a location:

**Patterns:**
| Structure | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Al + noun | *Al Fondale* | "at the bottom" |
| La + noun | *La Vasca* | "the basin" |
| Il + noun | *Il Bivio* | "the fork" |
| Casa + descriptor | *Casa Lupo* | "wolf house" |
| Ponte + descriptor | *Ponte Bassa* | "low bridge" |
| Studio + surname | *Studio Orsini* | owner's name |
| Cantina + descriptor | *Cantina Lunga* | "long cellar" |
| Acqua + colour | *Acqua Nera* | "black water" |

**Useful Italian nouns for places:** Arco (arch), Bivio (fork), Corda (rope), Ferro (iron), Fondo (bottom/depth), Gatta (cat), Muro (wall), Passo (pass), Piombo (lead), Porto (gate/entry), Rosso (red), Sasso (stone), Sorda (deaf/quiet), Stretto (narrow), Trave (beam), Vento (wind)

**Urban vocabulary (Venetian):**
| Word | What it describes |
|---|---|
| Calle | Narrow lane between buildings |
| Fondamenta | Canal-side walkway |
| Rio | Inner city canal |
| Campo | Small square |
| Campiello | Tiny square, barely room for a fountain |
| Sotoportego | Covered passage through a building |
| Ruga | Wider lane with shops on both sides |

Use these when placing a scene: *"You follow Fen into a calle barely wider than your shoulders, then out through a sotoportego onto a fondamenta."*

### Free Information (give if asked, DC 8–10 Persuasion)

Pull one when the party asks a canal-level NPC what they've noticed:

- The boat three moorings down hasn't moved in four days.
- A Canal Commission inspector was measuring a maintenance hatch at dawn with a knotted cord and no lantern.
- That Beffa team registered under a name that's a string of words in a Verdant Teeth dialect.
- One of the regular morning spot-checkers at the south anchorage hasn't shown up in two days.
- Three low hulls have been sitting in the outer roads with no pennant. Too clean for fishing boats.
- The watch sergeant at the Arsenal approach asks every boat the same question twice, slightly different each time.
- There's a buyer on the Bridge's top tier paying well for Red Lady survivor accounts.
- Nona Black-Jaw's kitchen door has been busy this week.
- A Warren Kin runner came through Le Paludi at an unusual hour.

## District Atmosphere

One sentence per district. Use when the party arrives somewhere new.

| District | Texture |
|---|---|
| Mercatura | Voices overlap; boats press in from both sides; the stone is warm from all-day sun. |
| The Bridge | The arch puts everyone in shadow; the vendors at the base haven't looked up all morning. |

## How Calvenans Talk

Every conversation in Calveno has a transaction under it, stated or implied. Even casual information creates a small social debt: *I told you, now you owe me a good opinion of this canal.*

**Common openings:**
- "Who sent you?" — before answering anything useful
- "That's not cheap." — before the price
- "Someone will have heard about this." — before helping with something private
- "Let's say—" — before a number
- "I know a man." / "I know a woman." — before a referral
- "Paid in full, witnessed, no argument." — closing a deal

**Common hedges:**
- "You understand I can't—" *(then does the thing, for a price)*
- "That's between you and whoever you're asking about." *(not a refusal; an invitation to make it worth their while)*
- "I only remember boats." *(deniability; they remember everything)*

**Tessarine voice (factors, clerks, envoys):**
More patient, more formal. They wait. They record. They don't rush.
- "I wonder if you've considered—" *(a better offer that serves them more)*
- "That's already in our ledger." *(they know more than you thought)*
- "The factor will want a note of this." *(surveillance, framed as helpfulness)*
- "Naturally, we'd be happy to facilitate—" *(a condition is coming)*

**Rattkin voice (Warren, Passage, Black-Jaw):**
Direct, concrete, no performance. Evaluate the situation, state the fact, name the price.
- "What do you need." *(not a question)*
- "That costs a favour." *(specific currency; they track these)*
- "Nona will know." *(sometimes a warning, sometimes a reassurance)*
