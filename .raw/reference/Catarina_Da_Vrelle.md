# Catarina Da Vrelle

## Summary

Catarina Da Vrelle is a young-looking artificer with white hair that betrays a mind older and busier than her face suggests. She operates out of Calveno as an artificer for hire, best known for her commissioned work with the Dravosi Crown — ship upgrades, soldier kit, practical engineering that keeps powerful people interested in keeping her safe. She holds her independence carefully, calls herself neutral, and mostly believes it. What she hasn't fully reckoned with is that neutrality has a price, and working for the Crown is what she'd name as the worst thing she's done — a compromise made with open eyes, for money and protection, that she doesn't much talk about.

Her Mortis is obvious to anyone who spends time with her: a mind that outpaces everything else, paired with a body that was clearly an afterthought. She's not built for the rough stuff and she knows it — sharp, insatiably curious, driven by a Da Vinci-esque hunger to understand and build everything. She can't sleep because her brain won't stop; turning over problems, sketching solutions, running calculations until dawn. What gets her out of bed despite the insomnia is the flying machine — the first one ever built without a single drop of magic. Not because she can't use it, but because she believes the journey matters. Magic, in her view, is a shortcut that steals the discovery. It's also exclusionary by nature, a gift hoarded by the born-lucky and the wealthy. Enchanted items distributed broadly — that's the way to give magic back to ordinary people. She's said this out loud, probably to the wrong crowds, more than once.

The ambition for flight wasn't born in a workshop. It was born in freefall — kidnapped as part of a raid, she escaped by jumping from cliffs into the water below while her father created a distraction. Whether he survived, she doesn't know for certain. She carries his flask of scotch, sealed, waiting for a celebration she still can't quite name. She also carries the mechanical owl she built early in her career — one of her first real creations, still ticking at her side, communicating in clicks and whirs. It doesn't talk. It doesn't need to.

Her father is the person whose opinion matters most to her, and his absence sits at the center of everything — the work ethic, the philosophy, the flask, the flying machine. She trusts the Dravosi Crown the way someone trusts a well-maintained tool: reliably, for as long as the utility holds. She treats everyone else — beggars, servants, children — the same as she'd treat anyone. No hierarchy. Probably something her father showed her by example.

The thing she doesn't know about herself is that she'd go further than she thinks to build the flying machine. She's convinced it's a pure pursuit — no magic, for the people, proof of what human ingenuity can achieve alone. She can't see yet how far that conviction might take her, or what she might compromise to get there. For a person who has thought carefully about almost everything, that blind spot is the most dangerous thing about her.

## Interview

**1. Let's start simple. What's their name? Any aliases, nicknames, titles they've picked up? And is there a name that only one specific person ever called them — something private?**
Catarina Da Vrelle. No aliases, nicknames, or private names.

**2. Before we get into anything else, paint me a picture. They walk through the door. What do I see?**
Youthful in appearance but with white hair — like Purah from Tears of the Kingdom. The kind of face that makes you do a double-take. Something intriguing before she even opens her mouth.

**3. What's their class? And I want to know *why* — is it a calling, something they chose, or something life didn't give them much of a choice about?**
Artificer — and it's a calling. She loves inventing. Da Vinci-inspired. The drive to understand and build everything is just who she is.

**4. Did they come into this with a Mortis — a flaw and a gift that are bound up together? If so, what's the Mark, and do *they* know it's there?**
She's a thinker, not a doer. Her Mortis gives her +2 INT in exchange for being physically weak and fragile. She knows it — she's just always been that way.

**5. What do they want right now, more than anything? And are they honest with themselves about it, or do they dress it up in something more acceptable?**
To build the first flying machine — without magical means. She's awestruck by the idea. She wants to prove that ingenuity alone can achieve what people reach for spells to do.

**6. By the end of all this — whatever this turns out to be — who do they want to *be*? Not what they want to accomplish. Who. Do they even know yet?**
Famous for her inventions, with a legacy that lasts generations. But it's not vanity — she wants to leave the world better than she found it. She's about the people. She wants her brilliance to have meant something.

**7. What keeps them up at night? Something real, not monsters. Is there something specific they're afraid of losing?**
Insomnia — she can't shut her brain off. She'd be afraid of losing her workshop, her projects, her business. It's not just property. It's her identity.

**8. What do they believe that most people around them would push back on? Have they ever actually said it out loud?**
That magic used willy-nilly is cheap — it steals the excitement of finding the possible within the impossible. It's a shortcut that kills the journey. Magic should be used for essentials, and learning to do things without it is far more valuable. Magic is exclusionary by default and should be shared with ordinary people via enchanted items they can use themselves. She's said it out loud. Probably to the wrong people. More than once.

**9. Is there a line they won't cross? And have they ever come close to it?**
She won't let her inventions be used to harm people or fall into the wrong hands. What she builds carries responsibility. She watched power get misused growing up — she decided early she'd never be that person.

**10. What did their life look like before all this? Day to day — what were they actually doing before they became an adventurer?**
She works in Calveno as an artificer for hire, primarily producing commissioned upgrades for Dravosi Crown ships and soldiers — while retaining her independence and neutrality. She's useful enough that they keep calling, careful enough that they've never quite owned her.

**11. What's the hardest thing they've had to survive — or do — to become the person we're meeting right now?**
She was caught up in a kidnapping raid — terrorists brought her to an island. Her father created a distraction so she could escape. She had to jump from cliffs into the water below to get out. Whether her father made it out with her is still an open question. She's the one who got away, and she carries that. The moment of freefall is also where her obsession with flight was born — she wants to make that choice on her own terms, next time.

**12. Do they carry something with a story? A weapon, a trinket, something worn down from use. What is it, and where did it come from?**
Two things. A mechanical owl — one of her earliest builds, still with her, communicating in clicks and whirs. It doesn't talk but it has its own language. And her father's flask, filled with good scotch, sealed, kept only for celebrations. She carries it because she doesn't know when the right moment is coming — and she wants to be ready.

**13. What's the worst thing they've ever done? Did they mean to do it?**
Working for the Dravosi Crown. She made the choice with open eyes — for the money, for the protection. She meant to do it. That's what makes it the worst thing.

**14. Whose opinion of them matters most? Where is that person now?**
Her father — a tinkerer like her. Presumably dead on the island she was kidnapped to and escaped from.

**15. Is there anyone out there who considers them an enemy? Who, and why?**
Not that she's aware of. But anyone with a grudge against the Dravosi probably sees her name on those ship upgrades and draws their own conclusions. She may have enemies she's never met.

**16. Who is the one person they'd trust with their life — not because that person is powerful, but just because they *trust* them? What did that person do to earn it?**
The Dravosi Crown — pragmatic trust. They need her alive and working, so they keep an eye on her. She's clear-eyed about what it is and doesn't dress it up as more than that. For someone like her, that reliability might be the most trustworthy thing in her life.

**17. How do they treat someone who can't do anything for them? A beggar, a servant, a child. Is that who they were raised to be, or something they had to figure out on their own?**
Kindly. Like any other human. No hierarchy in how she sees people — it tracks with everything she believes. Probably something her father modeled for her.

**18. What's the one thing they want so badly they might compromise who they are to get it? And do they know that about themselves?**
Flight. And no — she doesn't know it about herself. She's convinced it's a pure pursuit. She can't see the blind spot yet.
