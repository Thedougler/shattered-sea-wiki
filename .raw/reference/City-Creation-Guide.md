# City Creation Guide (Theme Park Method)

> Agent use: Read before creating a city, port, district, or location hub meant for play.

## Fast Rules

- Choose one legible theme before adding districts or lore.
- Design attractions players can interact with, not a lore museum.
- Each attraction needs activity, conflict, reward, and sensory identity.
- History matters only where it changes play at the table.


*Based on Pointy Hat's Theme Park Method.*

A city is a space players want to explore, not a lore classroom or backdrop for one plot beat. Design from a gameplay-first perspective.

## Core Philosophy

Think of a city as a theme park: a distinct identity with a variety of activities tailored to what the players at this specific table enjoy. Lore and history belong inside the experience, not as gates before it.

## Step 1: Choose a Theme

The theme is the city's visual and conceptual throughline. Everything should feel like it belongs to the same world defined by that theme.

**Good theme:** immediately visual, broad enough to support diverse content, specific enough to give distinct identity.

**Bad theme:** abstract or emotional ("Hope", "Love"), so [[Narrow]] it supports only one content type ("Blue", "Cat"), or generic.

Ask: what is this city *built on*, *obsessed with*, or *defined by*? The answer should be visible in architecture, economy, social hierarchy, and daily life.

### Theme Examples

**Undeath:** A metropolis-necropolis where class distinctions extend into death — what shroud you're buried in, whether you rest or labor as a skeleton. Necromancy is the most valued school of magic. True undeath (retaining personality) is an honor. The city is ruled by a council of liches.

**Hell:** Grew from a fortified paladin keep beside an uncloseable Hell portal. Trade with devils became a full economy. Mortals and devils may pass into each other's domains on official business. A hybrid culture and large tiefling/cambion population has developed from centuries of mingling.

**Underwater:** Built around a desert oasis that expanded underground into unexplored cave systems. The city is larger below the surface than above. Nobility and merchants live underwater; the working class lives in the sun above. Farming, mining, and galas all adapted to aquatic life.

**Holy:** A theocracy where the clergy took power after a cataclysm. Divine magic is king; the city monopolizes healing apparatuses that cure any wound, disease, or curse. Church and state are indistinguishable. Heresy is treason. Military is Paladin-based.

**Art:** Soft power as statecraft — when the world sings your songs and reads your novels, war becomes unpopular. Districts dedicated to painting, sculpture, dance, and theater, all intertwined with the arcane. Bardic Colleges dwarf wizarding ones in size and prestige. Fame and social connections are currency.

## Step 2: Build Attractions

Attractions are the things players can *do* in the city. Each maps to player preferences and fits the theme. Identify what players at this table enjoy first: high-stakes roleplay, combat, puzzle-solving, competitions, exploration, intrigue, stealth, loot.

**An attraction is most valuable when it appeals to more than one player simultaneously.**

| Category | Core Concept | Example Locations | Player Appeal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fighting Contest | Face teams of opponents for a prize | Colosseum, underground ring, stadium | Combat, loot |
| Infiltration | Enter a restricted area undetected | Jail, noble's estate, government building | Stealth, intrigue; add roleplay (disguise) or combat (caught) as variants |
| Ball / Social Event | Convince an attendee to align with party goals | Noble's estate, club | Roleplay, intrigue, Charisma builds |
| Competition | Contest of skill against participants for a prize | Stage, racing track, stadium | Skill builds, loot |
| Performance | Put on a show to earn money or get noticed | Theater, tavern, main square | Performance, roleplay, loot |
| Test of Mettle | Prove combat prowess to earn a job or ally | Barracks, fighting ring, training grounds | Combat |
| Dispatching an Enemy | Bring a dangerous city resident down | Masquerade ball, enemy's home, dark alley | Combat, intrigue, planning |
| Siege | Reinforce city defenses against an attacking force | City walls, central keep, government building | Combat, planning |
| Explore Uncharted Territory | Venture into secluded or forbidden areas | Sewers, ruins beneath the city, abandoned estate | Exploration, puzzles, combat |
| Open a Door | Solve a puzzle to unlock an inaccessible space | Wizard's tower, forbidden library wing | Exploration, puzzles |
| Gather Clues | Collect scattered evidence toward a destination or person | Contraband stashes, crime scene, city-wide trail | Exploration, intrigue, roleplay |

**Attractions can be combined.** Infiltration + Ball = some players mingle and gather intel while others run a stealth mission for blackmail evidence simultaneously.

Adapt every attraction to the city's theme. A Fighting Contest in an Art city is a battle of the bands; in a Hell city it's a duel run by a Pit Fiend.

Failure states should redirect the story or open new attractions — not dead-end.

## Design Checklist

- Theme is immediately visual and distinct
- Theme is present in architecture, economy, culture, and NPCs
- At least one attraction per player preference at this table
- Each attraction adapted to fit the theme
- Some attractions overlap in appeal (multiple players engaged at once)
- The city has something worth returning to after main plot beats resolve
