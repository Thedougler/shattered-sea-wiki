# Hunter

The iconic Ranger. Adds minimal complexity to the base class, complementing core tactics with mostly passive features. Only Superior Hunter's Defense requires additional action. Very accessible and easy to play—but most features provide minor benefits and won't be consistently useful. Decision points create illusion of choice rather than genuine tactical depth. Best in melee; ranged builds find little value here.

## Core Features

**Hunter's Lore** (3rd level)
Running raw/ingested/Hunters-Mark in nearly every encounter means you'll discover target Immunities/Resistances/Vulnerabilities. However, you deal Piercing damage almost exclusively, limiting options to exploit this knowledge. RAW interpretation suggests you also learn condition immunities (not reflected in Resistance/Vulnerability terminology).

**Hunter's Prey** (3rd level)
Choose one (can change on Short or Long Rest, but realistically you won't):

- **Colossus Slayer**: Enemies spend little time at full health; they'll be below full for the rest of combat. Deals 1d8 bonus damage when target is below max HP. Consistent, reliable, requires no effort. Works on Opportunity Attacks, making melee builds scarier to move past.

- **Horde Breaker**: Basically [[Cleave]] Weapon Mastery. Make an extra attack against a different creature within 5 feet if you hit your current target. Since you're locked onto raw/ingested/Hunters-Mark target and can't easily switch, opportunities to use this are vanishingly rare.

**Defensive Tactics** (7th level)
Choose one (can change on Short or Long Rest; you'll likely try Escape the Horde, then switch permanently to Multiattack Defense):

- **Escape the Horde**: Reduce Opportunity Attacks against you when you move away. Good for hit-and-run or ranged tactics. Multiattack Defense may still offer better overall protection.

- **Multiattack Defense**: Reduce damage from Multiattack. Nearly every enemy with weapon attacks has Multiattack by this level. Provides consistent defense in melee. Rarely useful at range.

**Superior Hunter's Prey** (9th level)
Add 1d6 damage per turn when attacking a creature different from your Hunter's Mark target (if a valid secondary target exists). Not mathematically significant. Works on Opportunity Attacks but doesn't make enough difference to matter.

**Superior Hunter's Defense** (15th level)
When you take damage from a source you can see, use your Reaction to halve the damage. Fantastic defensive option. Many enemies deal only one damage type per turn (dragon breath, spell, weapon), and multi-type damage dealers typically split effect types (mace + fire). This mitigates substantial damage.

## Optimization Notes

- **Colossus Slayer is your only real choice** at Hunter's Prey. Horde Breaker requires flexible targeting you don't have.
- **Multiattack Defense is your only real choice** at Defensive Tactics in actual combat. Escape the Horde is a trap unless you're deliberately drawing Opportunity Attacks.
- Melee builds benefit significantly from passive features; ranged builds may find little value outside Superior Hunter's Defense.
- The illusion of choice (Colossus vs. Horde Breaker, Escape vs. Multiattack) doesn't reflect meaningful tactical flexibility.
