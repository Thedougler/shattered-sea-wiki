# The Draves Bloodline Question

## The Situation

The Draves family name appears in Crown genealogical archives in sections that predate the current colonial administration by more generations than a family of their standing would normally explain. [[Aldric-Drave|Aldric Drave]] built the Crown's genealogical apparatus to track his bloodline. The Draves entry is in those records.

Whether Geoffrey is meaningfully connected to the sorcerous lineage — whether he is a functioning phylactery, whether his bloodline carries enough of the original potency to interest Aldric's heir-selection process — is deliberately unresolved. The surname similarity is one letter off from Drave. The archival depth is anomalous. Neither fact requires the other to be true.

**Leave this open. Do not commit either way.**

---

## The Aldous Angle

Aldous's factor work gives him routine access to Crown administrative archives: shipping licenses, noble precedents, commercial claims, inheritance filings. He has been reading these records for thirty years without paying close attention to the older sections.

He has noticed the depth of the Draves genealogical record. He has not questioned it, because the administrative culture he operates in treats deep genealogical documentation as a mark of legitimacy rather than a red flag. He does not know what the depth means. He filed it under "the family has been here a long time" and moved on.

---

## How It Surfaces

If the party searches deep Tidefall genealogical records, the Draves entry is there and it goes back further than expected. If Aldric Drave becomes aware that a Draves-branch descendant is disrupting Crown infrastructure alongside a foreign privateer crew, his interest is at least plausible.

Pull this thread when the campaign needs it. Let it lie until then.

## Geoffrey — DM Usage Notes

These are retained from the Geoffrey Draves secrets file. Nothing here has been resolved at the table.

**The dancing:** He practiced in the Surety's hold during quiet watches and is using the same approach now. He is getting worse at hiding it — not through carelessness, but through two years of not having a space to practice in. His body wants the movement back. He is less careful than he was on the Surety. Someone on this crew is going to see him eventually. The question is who and what they do with it.

**The vow — what it costs in play:** He has not been tested on it concretely. Everything so far has been inside the range of ordinary risk. The test arrives when the party's agenda and Geoffrey's agenda diverge into territory that costs him something he values: time, money, progress toward the goal, or the prospect of having a life after this ship. His vow was made for Verity's sake, not the party's. He has not chosen the party over his goal. He has chosen the party *as* his goal, for now.

**Uncle Knighton:** Do not escalate until [[Dorian-Bishop|Bishop]] is resolved. When Knighton becomes relevant, the party may try to use Geoffrey as a back-channel. Knighton does not owe Geoffrey anything. Blood connection may generate a hearing; it will not generate protection. Whether a fleet admiral would extend any professional consideration to a nephew who defected from a Crown vessel to work for foreign privateers is a question best left until there is something concrete riding on the answer.

**How to use Geoffrey at the table:**

*Low-pressure:* He is the crew member who can assess the ship's condition honestly. Give him small moments where his carpentry opinion matters — the hull before a crossing, a repair that needs doing, a choice between speed and safety. He earns trust this way without being pushed.

*Medium-pressure:* The dancing surfaces. Someone sees him. The party knows something he has not told them — a moment where Geoffrey decides whether to be embarrassed, defiant, or honest, and where players decide what they do with private information about a crewmate.

*High-pressure:* The party needs something in Tidefall, or Knighton becomes relevant, or the Aldric bloodline thread surfaces, or Verity's ledger problem becomes a party problem, or Geoffrey learns the full wage picture and has to decide what that means for his relationship with his father. These are all deferred. Each earns its moment.

*The vow test:* The party asks him to do something that costs him something toward his goal. He said any means. The campaign asks him to find out what that actually means in practice.
