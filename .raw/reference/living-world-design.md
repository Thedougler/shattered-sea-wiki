# Living World Design

> Agent use: Read before advancing factions, consequences, or off-screen pressure.

## Fast Rules

- Track what factions do if players ignore them.
- React honestly to player actions and absences.
- Consequences should arrive from faction logic, not author convenience.
- Use timelines as pressure, not railroad tracks.


The world doesn't pause while the players think. Villains plan, factions shift, and the world changes whether the players act or not. This indifference to player convenience is exactly what makes player choices feel real.

## The Core Tool: Faction Timelines

Before the campaign, sketch what major factions and NPCs are doing now and what they will do next if nobody interferes. A handful of bullets per faction:

- Current state
- Next action if players do nothing (and timeframe)
- Resources and knowledge available to them

Example: "Cultists are three weeks from completing the ritual." "Trade guild is one bad harvest from turning on the city council." "Rival party is already looking for the same artifact."

## Honest Reactions

When the party interacts with a faction, react honestly to what they actually did — not to what would be convenient for the story. Expose the guild's corruption? It retaliates or buys protection. Ignore a faction for several sessions? It has continued pursuing its goals.

Intelligent villains act intelligently — they prepare, adapt, and use what they know. A villain who has watched the party long enough to know their patterns is more dangerous than one who merely knows their class levels.

## Stage Set vs. Living World

**Stage set:** pauses when players aren't watching. Everything exists for them. Nothing happens unless they trigger it.

**Living world:** indifferent to player convenience. Factions pursue goals independently. Players arrive to consequences, not just opportunities.
