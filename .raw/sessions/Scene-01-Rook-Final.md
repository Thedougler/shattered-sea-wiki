# The Rook Resolution

*Opens mid-combat. [[Barnaby-Rook|Barnaby Rook]] hauled himself back aboard the [[HCS-Surety]] after being blasted off the gangplank. He fired his flintlock at [[Beaumont-Sel|Beaumont]] — round deflected off the shell. Beaumont entered the fight. The [[Dravosi-Alchemist|Alchemist]] and [[Capn-Gorgeous|Enforcer]] are dead. [[Geoffrey-Draves|Geoffrey Draves]] is on the party's side. Rook has been watching all of it from the cutter deck. He knows exactly what he's facing.*

## Before He Falls — Checklist

This fight has a shape. Don't let it end before the shape is complete.

- [ ] **All three lair actions have fired** in sequence: Arm the Guns → Fire the Guns → Call to Arms.
- [ ] **Call to Arms fired and was refused.** Grigori's voice came up from below. Rook knows he is alone.
- [ ] **At least one PC has been downed or shoved into the water.** Beaumont has potions; this isn't fatal. It just needs to happen.
- [ ] **At least 3 full rounds of initiative have elapsed.**

If the party's damage is outpacing the checklist, use Parry on every triggering hit. See the Dial for mid-fight levers.

## The Fight

Rook is not standing in place waiting to absorb seven attacks. He spent two phases watching from the cutter deck. He knows who's hurt, who's dangerous, who the weak link is. He has a plan for the first two rounds at minimum, and he uses every inch of his ship.

### Round 1 — The Opening

**Free action:** *"Uncover the lanterns."* No crew to do it — he does it himself, kicking the hood off the nearest hooded lantern as he moves. Bright light floods his end of the deck. [[content/shattered-sea/private/players/Jean-Claude-Tabarnack|Jean-Claude]]'s Umbral Sight collapses anywhere within the lantern's 20-ft. radius. If Rook can reach a second lantern on his first move, he kills it for the whole deck.

**No pistol** (unless the DM decides otherwise). He has fired at Beaumont and through the hatch. He leads with his cutlass. He crosses the deck fast and opens into melee rather than a ranged shot.

**Naval Footwork:** He Disengages as a bonus action and moves to a position where at most two PCs can reach him. He is not getting swarmed.

### Round 2+ — The Fight

Two Cutlass attacks per turn, using the shove option whenever a PC is near the gangplank edge or the rail. He does not stand still. Every turn he reassesses and repositions — he Disengages after attacking, forces the party to spend movement chasing him across his own deck. He uses the rigging and cargo nets as cover, corners where only one PC can engage him at a time.

**Targeting priority:**
1. Whoever looks most injured — he's trying to drop someone fast to change the math
2. [[content/shattered-sea/private/players/Perrin-Black-Jaw|Perrin]] — softest target, ranged threat that needs silencing
3. Whoever is closest to the gangplank edge — shove them in, buy a round

### Legendary Actions

Rook has 3 legendary actions per round, spent at the end of other creatures' turns. He regains all at the start of his turn.

| Action | Cost | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Reposition** | 1 | Move up to half speed without provoking opportunity attacks. Use this to stay ahead of the party's positioning, not to escape. He is not running. |
| **Cutlass Strike** | 2 | One Cutlass attack with the shove option on a hit. Use between PC turns to threaten anyone who thinks the gap between their turn and his is safe. |
| **Pistol Reload** | 2 | Reload one expended flintlock — does not fire. Sets up a ranged option on his next turn. Worth using if the party is keeping their distance. |

He will spend legendary actions reactively, not by rotation. Reposition whenever a PC closes a gap he doesn't want closed. Cutlass Strike when someone is near the gangplank edge and he has 2 actions to spend. Pistol Reload only if the party is playing ranged.

## Lair Actions — Round Arc

On initiative count 20 each round (losing ties), Rook issues a command. He cannot use the same lair action two rounds in a row. Run them in this sequence.

### Round 1 — Arm the Guns (init count 20)

Rook bellows the order below. No crew appears, but the gun port hatches scrape open from below — someone down there still follows orders when it doesn't require stepping on deck. The two amidship cannon ports are uncovered. No immediate effect. The guns are primed for next round.

> [!DM]
> No save, no damage — this is setup. Its purpose is to put the table on notice that something is coming.
>
> **Beaumont's response — Tidefall Maneuver:** On his turn this round, Beaumont uses the [[Beaumont-Sel|Tidefall Maneuver]]. He makes a light underhand toss and [[Bisou]] launches through the air, lands at the nearest exposed gun port, and soaks the powder in the chamber — using her own means if no water is within reach. One gun is disabled for the rest of the fight. No save; the gun can't dodge. Beaumont loses his action but the party loses one cone of fire in Round 2.
>
> This is automatic. Beaumont knows exactly what Arm the Guns means and he acts the moment the ports open.

### Round 2 — Fire the Guns (init count 20)

One cannon discharges — the one Bisou didn't reach. It fires in a 30-ft. cone extending from its gun port along the weather deck. Every creature in the cone makes a **DC 14 Dexterity save**, taking **4d10 bludgeoning** on a fail, half on a success.

> [!DM]
> Still the fight's biggest single threat — one cone rather than two, but the average (22 on a fail) will still drop most PCs at this level. Position matters: anyone who has been chasing Rook toward the amidships section is in the kill zone.
>
> Beaumont is slow (15 ft.) and may still be closing when the gun fires. His Antheri Plate gives disadvantage on ranged attacks, not area saves — the cannon bypasses it. He can take it.
>
> Use Cutlass Strike as a legendary action this round. The party will be scrambling after the volley.

### Round 3 — Call to Arms (init count 20)

Rook grabs the bell line and hauls. Once. Twice. Three times. The iron note rings out sharp and clean over dark water.

He waits.

The ship breathes below. Timber settles. No boots on the ladder. No voice called up.

Then, muffled through the hatch, flat and final: *"No."*

His jaw tightens. He draws his pistol and puts a round through the door frame.

*"You shot me?! Fuck you, Brataby!"*

Footsteps move away into the hull. A long pause. They return.

*"The crew says they quit. I can't blame them. Hostile work environment."*

Nobody comes up. The lair action fails.

> [!DM]
> The voice is [[Shepherd-Grigori|Shepherd Grigori]]. The round hit the door frame — Grigori is a CR 19 Hierarch, the splinter barely registered. He says "You shot me?!" the way a man says it when he cannot believe the audacity. "Fuck you Brataby" is genuine irritation. Then he walks away, checks with the crew, comes back. *"The crew says they quit"* lands in his usual register: warm, deliberate, completely unruffled.
>
> This is the moment the table should feel his situation. He is a bad captain. His crew hate him. Even now, with their ship being taken, they won't fight for him.
>
> Rook continues fighting. He's pragmatic, not dramatic. But something shifts in his posture. He stops calling orders. He just swings.
>
> **HP threshold:** If Rook is above 55 HP when Round 3 arrives, delay Call to Arms — use Reposition instead and run it as soon as he crosses the threshold. It doesn't have to land on exactly Round 3, but it must land.

### Geoffrey Crisis

Once, on any turn Rook chooses, he calls out to Geoffrey by name — free action, no cost.

*"Draves. You swore an oath. Look at what you're standing next to."*

Geoffrey makes a DC 12 Wisdom saving throw (roll it openly). On a failure, he spends his next action doing nothing: standing, hand on his sword, unable to commit. He recovers the following turn. This costs Rook nothing and removes an actor for a round. Use it when the action economy feels lopsided, not as the first thing he does.

## Terrain

**The cutter deck** is ~40×15 ft., clear of cargo. Two hooded lanterns — one at bow, one at stern. Rook knows the geometry cold.

**The gangplank** connects the Surety and Saltwright. Any creature within 5 ft. of the gangplank edge that takes the cutlass shove option must make a DC 12 Dexterity save or go over the side — dark water between the hulls, 10 ft. drop. A creature in the water needs to spend its action each turn making a DC 10 Athletics check to stay afloat and move toward a ladder. Climbing back up costs the rest of its movement.

**The rail** runs the length of the cutter deck. Same rule as the gangplank edge for shoving — Rook knows where the weaker sections are.

**Rigging:** Three shroud lines accessible from the deck. A creature can use its action to climb the rigging (DC 10 Athletics) and gain elevation, but it's exposed and loses melee cover.

## Rook's Gambit — The Net Trap

*Rook had time to rig a weighted cargo net on a slip-knot targeting airborne approach. If not triggered in Session 01, it is still live.*

**Trigger:** [[Crissdalynn-Khinriss|Crissdalynn]] flies or leaps over the gap between the ships, or over Rook's position.
**Save:** DC 12 Dexterity or Restrained. On a fail she drops onto the deck, netted.
**Net:** AC 10, 10 HP, immune to bludgeoning and poison. Slashing damage only.

Alternatively, if the trap feels redundant or already triggered, Rook can cut the net rope himself on his turn, dropping it across the gangplank mouth as difficult terrain (costs a bonus action, 5 ft. radius of tangled net). A creature moving through it halves its speed and must succeed on a DC 10 Dexterity save or fall prone.

## Roleplay Off-Ramp

Rook is pragmatic, not suicidal. But he's had a bad night and he is not offering terms first. That ship has sailed. He'll negotiate only if the party forces it.

**Threshold:** ≤40 HP remaining (not 50%). He has to feel it before he'll talk.

**What opens him:** A DC 14 Persuasion attempt, with something real behind it: leverage, a face-saving offer, proof that killing him makes things worse. *"The Crown knows this ship's route. Someone will come looking."* That works. Generic threats don't.

**What he'll give:** Let the Saltwright go, manifest goes unreported, Surety pulls away. He keeps his ship. He does not forget this — but he is professional enough not to make it personal today.

**What he won't give:** Names from other inspections. The Crown's internal business. Anything that costs him more than this one run.

If the party kills him instead, they own the Surety and everything becomes harder at the next Crown checkpoint.

## Dial

### The Floor

If Rook would drop to 0 HP before the checklist is complete, he drops to 1 HP instead. This happens **once**. Call it: he caught the blow on his coat, spun with the momentum, didn't go down cleanly. He's been taking hits on this route for years. It doesn't hold — but it buys the next round and keeps the arc intact.

---

### Party Configuration

Check who is at the table before the session starts. The statblock is calibrated for five PCs.

**5 PCs (Stripes present):** Run as written. ~37 gross DPR vs. ~26 net after Parry. Expected fight length at HP 120, AC 17: ~4–5 rounds. The floor mechanic handles variance.

**4 PCs (Stripes absent):** Drop HP to 110 and lower the negotiation threshold to 45 HP. Net DPR falls to ~21 — at full HP 120 the fight would run 5–6 rounds, which is too long. With HP 110 and an earlier off-ramp it sits at ~4 rounds with a clean exit before it drags. Do not reduce AC — that number is doing real work on individual hit probability.

> [!DM]
> If you don't know until people sit down, default to the 5-PC statblock. It's easier to shorten a fight than extend one — the off-ramp and the floor give you both directions in real time.

### Mid-Fight Levers

Use these in real time. The goal is for the fight to feel dangerous without TPKing a party that slaughtered his entire crew.

**Rook is going down too fast:**

- **Parry everything.** The reaction has no per-round limit. It just requires holding a melee weapon and being aware of the attacker. Use it on every hit that would push him below the next checklist milestone. Average reduction is 11.5 (1d10+6); that's a meaningful speed bump on nearly any attack.
- **Delay Call to Arms.** If Rook hasn't crossed 55 HP yet but is close, play the lair action cautiously: use Reposition instead of burning Call to Arms early. Save it for when the threshold is actually crossed so the narrative beat lands at the right moment.
- **Geoffrey hesitates.** Use the crisis call one round earlier than planned. Even on a successful save, describe Geoffrey's hand slowing on his sword grip for a half-second. It costs the party a beat of attention without mechanically freezing him.
- **Rook goes vertical.** He climbs to the rigging (DC 10 Athletics, costs his action). The party has to choose whether to follow or let him hold the high ground. Melee characters have to spend movement; Jean-Claude and Crissdalynn can follow easily. Creates a positioning problem worth one round.

**Rook is surviving too long / the party is struggling:**

- **Beaumont heals.** The Kalowe Maneuver delivers a 2d4+2 potion to any downed or bloodied ally within 30 ft. as Beaumont's action. He has three potions. Use this freely. It's the safety valve the fight was designed around. A downed PC being revived mid-fight is drama, not a failure state.
- **Rook drops a shove.** He attacks but doesn't attempt the gangplank push this round — a clean miss on the geometry, or he was already moving. One less secondary hazard.
- **Geoffrey steadies.** He flanks with a PC, granting advantage on their attack. Doesn't require a scene, just describe it.
- **Rook opens negotiation early.** Drop the threshold to 55 HP instead of 40. He's pragmatic enough to count — if the fight's going badly, he'd rather walk away.

## Resolution Outcomes

**Rook defeated:** The party controls the [[HCS-Surety]]. Rook is dead, unconscious, or taken prisoner. Move to [[Scene-02b-Deck-Confrontation]].

**Rook negotiates:** He gives ground: manifest goes dark, Saltwright goes free, Surety pulls away. He keeps his ship. Move to [[Scene-02b-Deck-Confrontation]] on the Saltwright deck.

**Rook escapes:** He goes over the side. He can swim, it's his route, he knows which rocks to avoid. The Surety pulls away. Move to [[Scene-02b-Deck-Confrontation]] on the Saltwright deck. Rook is a future problem.
