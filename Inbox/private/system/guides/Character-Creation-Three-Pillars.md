# Character Creation: Three Pillars Method

> Agent use: Read before helping create or evaluate a player character concept.

## Fast Rules

- Start with gimmick, conflict, and want before mechanics.
- Conflict must be personal, actionable, and campaign-facing.
- Want gives the character forward motion; backstory explains why.
- Use the three pillars to find hooks, not to lock an arc.


Pointy Hat's system-agnostic framework for building a character's core identity, motivation, and arc potential before touching any mechanical choices. Covers motivation, backstory, and central conflict — not class, abilities, or spells.

## Pillar I — Gimmick

The most recognizable and most shallow aspect of who the character is. Establishes a foundation for a more dimensional character and creates an instant, legible impression at the table.

The Gimmick is a clear point of view — something another player can grasp immediately.

**Examples:** Quick to Anger, Scaredy-Cat, Jokester, Absolute Idiot, Know-it-All, Excessively Naive, Prissy Noble, Morbid and Creepy, Compulsive Liar.

## Pillar II — Conflict

The main issue the character is currently facing. Conflict must be personal to the character rather than vaguely related to them. It provides direction for character growth and drives backstory construction toward arcs that can develop through play.

**Examples:** Being persecuted for a crime they didn't commit; evading authorities; being disowned by family; losing a loved one; suffering a great injustice; being the subject of a prophecy; pursuing an unsolved mystery.

## Pillar III — Want

The character's deepest desire at the start of their story. A character with a Want has agency — they seek things out rather than having the story happen to them. The Want can evolve into a **Need** through play: something related to but distinct from the original Want, shaped by what the character has experienced. A character who wants to be accepted by their parents may come to need to accept themselves.

**Examples:** Find out who my parents are; be accepted by my family; become strong enough to need no one; make those who wronged me suffer; rule my father's kingdom; become rich; bring a loved one back from the dead.

## How the Pillars Interact

- **Gimmick** is the surface — what other players see first.
- **Conflict** is the wound or external pressure — what the character is up against.
- **Want** is the engine — what the character is reaching toward.

Together they set up a character arc: the Want defines the goal, the Conflict creates the obstacle, and the Gimmick is the behavior pattern the character must eventually grow beyond (or lean into) to resolve both.
