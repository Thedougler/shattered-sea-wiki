# Dungeon Creation Method (Pointy Hat)

> Agent use: Read before generating a dungeon, ruin, lair, or enclosed adventure site.

## Fast Rules

- Pick one theme; make every mechanic and room express it.
- Define the dungeon goal before mapping encounters.
- Encounters should test choices, not just drain resources.
- Endings should reveal consequence, cost, or transformation.


A system-agnostic 5-step framework for building thematically cohesive dungeons. Written with 5e in mind. Focuses on thematic resonance over room counts or encounter balance.

## The Method

### Step I: Theme

The most important step — all other steps derive from it. Define the theme in **one word** to avoid convolution. A theme sets the mood and determines setting (what the dungeon is, where it is, how it came to be).

> Examples: *lies* → a carnival of deception; *healing* → an abandoned hospital; *death* → a noble family mausoleum.

### Step II: Mechanics

Select game mechanics that express the theme — rules you can tweak, use, or introduce to make players *feel* the theme through gameplay, not just flavor.

> Examples: *lies* → illusion magic, Insight/Perception checks; *healing* → HP regeneration, hit dice, healing magic; *death* → necromancy, death saving throws, undead enemies.

### Step III: Goal

Define what success looks like — what players must achieve to complete the dungeon. Vary goals across a campaign to maintain engagement.

> Examples: *lies* → rescue an NPC trapped under enchantment; *healing* → recover a soul-healing potion recipe; *death* → defeat the undead patriarch commanding his reanimated kin.

### Step IV: Encounters

Encounters are planned events divided into three types:

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Combat** | Fighting encounters using theme mechanics |
| **Puzzle / Trap** | Environmental challenges expressing the theme |
| **Roleplay** | Social or narrative encounters tied to theme |

Vary encounter types for pacing and to explore the theme from multiple angles.

> Examples: *lies* → combat in a hall of mirrors; *healing* → convincing a ghost patient they're dead; *death* → puzzle where only a dying PC can see the true path.

### Step V: Ending

The climax. Synthesize all prior steps into a final encounter that uses every mechanic and aspect of the theme introduced through the dungeon.

> Examples: *lies* → players must lie to the enchanted NPC to lead them out; *healing* → defeat a creature by healing it to a threshold rather than damaging it; *death* → only a PC currently rolling death saves can slay the patriarch in the realm of the dead.

## Design Principles

- Theme drives every decision — setting, mechanics, encounters, and ending must all reinforce it.
- Express theme mechanically, not just descriptively; players should *interact* with the theme.
- A single-word theme prevents scope creep.
- The ending is a culmination: bring together all mechanics and theme expressions used previously.
