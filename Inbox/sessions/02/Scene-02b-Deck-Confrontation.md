# Scene 02b — The Reckoning on the Surety

*Immediately after [[Scene-01-Rook-Final]] resolves. Before [[Scene-03-Grigori]].*

*[[Barnaby-Rook|Rook]] is down. The fight is over. [[Beaumont-Sel|Beaumont Sel]] lowers the boat hook. He looks at [[content/shattered-sea/characters/player/Perrin-Black-Jaw|Perrin]] and [[content/shattered-sea/characters/player/Jean-Claude-Tabarnack|Jean-Claude]].*

---

## When to Run This Scene

Run this whenever Rook's resolution ends with the party on the Surety's deck — defeat, negotiation, or escape. It works in all three outcomes. If Rook escapes over the side, Beaumont still turns to address the stowaways before the deck clears. The stakes are just lower.

**Skip it only if:** the session is running long and you need to reach [[Scene-03-Grigori]] fast. In that case, compress to a single line from Beaumont and move on: *"We'll talk. But not here. Come below."*

---

## What Beaumont Knows

- [[content/shattered-sea/characters/player/Perrin-Black-Jaw|Perrin]] is a Rattkin who stowed away on his ship, the *[[Saltwright]]*. He knows what Rattkin stowaways mean. He is a Friend of the [[The-Passage|Passage]] — a Rattkin underground network running hidden routes under the Overland Track on Calder's Tooth. He would have sheltered Perrin without question if asked.
- [[content/shattered-sea/characters/player/Jean-Claude-Tabarnack|Jean-Claude]] is a blue Grung. Blue caste in Grung society: scouts, messengers, handlers of slaves. The advance eye before the raid comes. Beaumont has run the [[content/shattered-sea/places/midchain/index|Midchain]] for eleven years. He has seen what a blue-caste presence in a port means in practice. He is more afraid of Jean-Claude than he was of Barnaby Rook. That fear is old and earned.
- He has a [[Truth-Stone|Truth Stone]] in his coat. He uses it as a matter of course when a conversation carries stakes. It is not a threat. It is a tool.

---

## Opening — Post-Fight Atmosphere

> [!READ-ALOUD]
> The fight is over. The Surety's deck settles into itself, timber creaking, rigging catching a wind that doesn't know the night has changed. Spent powder still clings to the salt air. Dark water presses at the strakes below, slow and indifferent. Barnaby Rook is down.
>
> Beaumont Sel lowers the boat hook. He doesn't look at Rook. He looks at you.
>
> Bisou goes still on his shoulder. Her amber eyes move across the deck. She has been counting.

---

## Beat 1 — Gratitude

> [!READ-ALOUD]
> He takes the clay pipe out of his mouth and tucks it into his vest pocket. It is a small gesture, deliberate, the kind a man makes when he wants to be understood.
>
> "You fought for this ship." The patois comes out slow, no hurry in it. "For me and mine. I don't forget that."
>
> His eye moves. It finds Perrin first.

---

## Beat 2 — Perrin

> [!READ-ALOUD]
> He takes in the hood, the three-foot frame, the way a Rattkin stands when they already know where the exits are.
>
> "Know what you are," he says. Not unkind. "Know why a Kin would tuck themselves in a hold rather than knock on a door." He lets that sit. "I'm a Friend of the Passage. You didn't need to be a ghost on my ship. Any Kin in need, you ask. That is all. You ask."
>
> He holds Perrin's eyes long enough to land it. Then his eye moves.
>
> It finds Jean-Claude.

> [!DM]
> Perrin needs no check here. Beaumont is not testing him — he is telling him something. The scene is not about Perrin. Let Perrin's player absorb it, then move on.

---

## Beat 3 — Jean-Claude (Tone Shift)

> [!READ-ALOUD]
> The warmth in his face doesn't leave. It settles. Gets heavier. Something behind that one visible eye that knows the shape of a specific kind of trouble, the kind that comes ahead of worse trouble, and has known it for eleven years on these waters.
>
> Bisou tilts her head. Just slightly. She is watching the Grung too.
>
> "Blue," Beaumont says. He says it the way a man names a weather system he has been watching build on the horizon. "Know what blue means. Scout caste. Handler caste. The ones who come ahead of the rest to see what there is to take." His voice stays level. The level costs him something, you can tell. "So I'm going to ask you direct, and I would appreciate the same back. Where is the party you're riding ahead of? And is this night done, or isn't it?"
>
> He reaches into his coat. Produces a smooth grey stone, unremarkable, except for the faint shimmer of heat around it in the cool night air. He tosses it, soft and easy, across the deck toward Jean-Claude.
>
> "Hold that."
>
> He cracks his knuckles. Rolls his shoulders. His eye drops to the stone in Jean-Claude's hand, and he waits.

> [!DM]
> **The Truth Stone.** When a creature holding it makes a Deception check, pass or fail, it deals 1d6 fire damage per turn for 1 minute, stacking with each check. Heats to white-hot when a deliberate lie is spoken. Dropping it ends all damage immediately.
>
> Jean-Claude will feel nothing. His Mortis flaw gives him permanent disadvantage on Deception. He cannot mask himself, his intentions, or what he is. His face gives him away before he finishes the sentence. The stone is a formality he passes without effort.
>
> **Beaumont's fear:** Do not play this as prejudice against Jean-Claude as an individual. It is specific, operational knowledge. He has run the Midchain for eleven years. He knows what a blue-caste advance scout means in practice. He is not wrong to ask — he just happens to be asking the one Grung in the Shattered Sea who is not what he fears. Let that land with the table.
>
> **Bisou:** She is reading the room. She relaxes at the exact moment Beaumont does. The players will notice, even if no one says so out loud.

---

## Beat 4 — Jean-Claude's Answer

> [!DM]
> Let the player speak. The honest answer: Jean-Claude is a deserter from the Botukuri clan of the [[Verdant-Teeth|Verdant Teeth]]. He left because of what the clan did to a gnome named [[Pell|Pell]]. He is not scouting for anyone. He is not a forward eye for a raid. He is travelling alone, or as close to it as a three-foot blue Grung in a fake moustache can manage.
>
> The stone stays cool through all of it.
>
> If the player leaves something important out, Beaumont asks, calm and specific: *"You understand what I'm asking. Is there a party. Yes or no."*
>
> He is not trying to trap Jean-Claude. He is trying to survive the next week. There is a difference, and he knows it.
>
> **If Jean-Claude attempts a Deception check for any reason:** The stone fires immediately. Beaumont's voice goes flat and quiet: *"Don't."* He does not get angry. He just stops trusting. The scene can recover but the dynamic shifts.

---

## Beat 5 — Acceptance

> [!READ-ALOUD]
> When the last word of it is out, Beaumont is quiet. His eye goes from the stone in Jean-Claude's hand to the Grung's face, and it stays there for a long moment.
>
> Whatever he finds, he believes it.
>
> "Right then." Same voice. The weight in it shifts. It doesn't disappear, but it changes shape, the way an old fear does when it doesn't get the answer it was braced for. He takes the pipe out of his vest pocket and settles it back in the corner of his mouth. "Either of you needs passage, you ask. That goes for both of you."
>
> He glances across the Surety's deck. There is still work in this night.
>
> "Now. Let's see what we've taken."

> [!DM]
> **Transition:** Move directly to [[Scene-03-Grigori]]. The scene ends when Beaumont turns away.
>
> **What this establishes:** Beaumont trusts both stowaways — not completely, not yet, but enough for passage. He is a Friend of the Passage where Perrin is concerned; where Jean-Claude is concerned, he is a man who asked the hard question, got a hard answer, and accepted it. That's its own kind of respect. The relationship between all three has a foundation to build on.
