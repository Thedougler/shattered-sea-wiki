# Below Deck

*This scene fires once, immediately after [[Scene-01-Rook-Final]] resolves with the party controlling the Surety. Trigger on the first character who goes below.*

The smell reaches you at the ladder. Something slow-cooked, heavy with aromatics, filling a dank space that has no business smelling this good. The stove ticks with heat, its iron side beaded with moisture from whatever has been simmering on it for the last hour. Someone has been down here making something out of a Crown patrol galley's stores.

The galley is lit. One man is at the table, and his presence fills the low-ceilinged space the way a fire fills a room. Dark hair loose to his shoulders, a thick wiry beard to match. A coat of deep red and gold, salt-stiff at the cuffs but unmistakably the coat of a senior Dravosi Naval Officer, the sleeves rolled up against protocol. A bottle of fine old wine sits in front of him, already well begun. Several places have been set at the table, as if he was expecting company.

He is tying off a bandage on his forearm, one-handed, without slowing the meal. He looks up when you enter, and his eyes find you in the lamplight. Dark. Very still. Entirely unsurprised. His face cracks into a warm smile. 

*"So the ship is under new management?"*

He asks it the way you ask if the kitchen is still serving. He gestures to the places he has set at the table.

*"You must be starving. Sit. The crew has some questions."*

## Shepherd Grigori

> [!DM]
> [[Shepherd-Grigori\|Shepherd Grigori]] is a CR 19 undead Hierarch sorcerer. He is warm, deliberate, and completely unfrightened. The ease is not an act. Nothing in this room threatens him. He is here because Rook was transit, not purpose. He is mildly irritated about the arm (the word "Brataby" came out with genuine feeling) but he has moved past it, and visibly. He is heading to Calveno where a young heir the Crown physicians gave up on is waiting for a miracle. The party is an inconvenience that has become slightly more interesting. He has no plans to hurt anyone unless they make it necessary.
>
> **The faith is real.** He is modelled on the Khlystý, a mystic sect that treats excess as devotion, pleasure as prayer, and the body as the instrument through which the divine is reached. The meal is not an affectation. He made something real out of a galley's stores because he takes pleasure seriously, and because this is what his faith looks like in practice. Play this straight. The warmth is genuine. The holiness is genuine. The healing is genuine. The horror is that he also considers the binding genuine: he healed them, they are part of him now, and he sees this as a fair and natural exchange between shepherd and flock. He is not wrong about the healing. He is wrong about everything that follows from it.
>
> The crew stayed behind the door because they were afraid. Grigori stayed at the table because he wasn't. When the crew asked who should talk to the pirates, everyone looked at him and he said *"Fine"* and refilled his glass. He considers this mildly inconvenient and entirely beneath him. He does not show it.

**Who he is, if asked:** *"A shepherd. I go where people have problems that cannot be fixed. And I fix them."* Pressed: stories of courts, of grateful families, of a young heir in Calveno whose physicians had given up. Warm. Specific. Verifiable, if anyone cared to check. He tells these stories the way a man tells them who has many more and could keep going all night.

**About the wound:** *"A misunderstanding. Nothing worth discussing."* He does not elaborate. He ties the last knot and rolls his sleeve back down.

**About the fight:** *"I heard. These things happen at sea."*

**About the bell / the hatch:** *"He rang for All Hands. I declined on behalf of the crew."* A pause. *"He was not pleased."* He refills his wine.

**About Rook:** Nothing positive. Nothing angry. The absence of feeling is more specific than dislike would be.

## Crew Beat — Murrat

*Fires naturally once the crew is at the table. Can be triggered by any Rook question, or just let it come up organically when there's a lull.*

The crew's feelings about Rook are not complicated. Old Faas puts it plainly, without being asked, somewhere between the first course and the second:

*"He took us to Murrat."*

A beat. He says it the way you report a weather event you survived.

*"Wanted a live one. A specimen, he called it. Went ashore with six crew. Four came back needing the surgeon."*

Alys doesn't look up from whatever she's doing with her hands.

*"Severe puncture wounds. Blood loss. Two of them I genuinely wasn't sure about."*

She pauses.

*"Shepherd Grigori sat with them through the night. I did what I could, but." She sets her hands flat on the table. "They pulled through because of him. I'll say that plainly."*

Grigori, if he's still at the table, does not respond to this. He refills his wine.

> [!DM]
> Don't push the Grigori angle. Let it sit. The crew believes he saved those sailors — because he did — and the warmth they have for him is genuine and slightly uncomfortable given what he is. Alys is a competent surgeon who knows when something was beyond her. She is not wrong to credit him. She doesn't know what the credit means.

## The Question

Once the party confirms they're in charge, Grigori sets down his fork.

*"Then I have a question. If you will indulge me."*

He refills his wine without asking if anyone wants some. He is not in a hurry.

*"In my travels I have met three kinds of pirates. The first kind. Strange ones. Their plans make no sense. They take things you would not think to value. Unpredictable. They almost never hurt people by accident, but only because hurting people is the goal."*

He rotates the glass once, considering.

*"The second kind are businessmen. Hostages, cargo, passage fees. Pure mathematics. They prefer you alive because you are worth more. Very clean thinking. I can respect it."*

*"And then there are the robin hood ones."* A slight pause. *"These have a code. They keep their word when it costs them something. They are embarrassed by their crew's bad behavior, which I find charming, actually. Not a common trait in the profession."*

He says, quieter, almost to the wine: *"I am always glad to meet the third kind. There are fewer of them than there should be."*

He looks at the party. Genuinely curious.

*"I ask because the answer tells me what kind of cook I should be."*

> [!DM]
> This is intelligence gathering in a dinner party coat. He wants to know how dangerous they are in ways that affect his plans. Any real answer works. He just needs the baseline. Silence or deflection is the only wrong move.
>
> Let the table discuss it. Let them argue about which type they are. That conversation is the scene.
>
> If they ask which type he is: *"I am a guest. The question does not apply."*

## His Condition

Before any deal is done, he raises it without pressure. Not a demand, just an expectation that reasonable people share:

*"One thing only. The crew. Anyone who wants to leave at the next port, you let them leave. They have had a very particular kind of week."*

He does not negotiate it. He does not threaten if refused. He simply notes it the way you note something obvious, and moves on. He means it. The care for the crew is not political. He is their shepherd in this moment as genuinely as he is anyone's, and he does not draw a sharp line between people who have paid for his services and people who happened to be on the same ship.

**If the party refuses:** The warmth doesn't leave all at once. It just... thins. He says *"That's unfortunate"* and waits to see if they reconsider. He is not in a hurry. He does not raise his voice. He has enough patience for this and considerably more besides.

## The Pretty One

Before the Calveno question, something occurs to him. He asks it the same way he asked about the wine.

*"The tall one. Long hair. Looked like a statue."*

He waits.

*"Is he still alive?"*

When the answer is no:

He sets his glass down. 

*"Mm."* A pause. *"That is a shame."*

He taps the rim once with one finger.

*"House Knighton. Very old family. Well connected in the Navy. His father will hear his son died on this patrol, and the vessel is now missing. Commodore Knighton does not solve his problems alone. He sends his own fleet."* A beat. *"The Knights. Those ships do not weigh themselves down with brigs."*

He picks up his glass again.

*"So."*

*"[[Kalowe]]. No Crown presence, no Knighton contacts I am aware of. Five days for the work, perhaps. [[Calveno]] has better facilities. Two days, maybe less. If your going to Calveno that would be good for me, I have business there."*

He does not say which he would choose.

> [!DM]
> Grigori does not know Cap'n Gorgeous's real name. He recognized the Knighton house crest on the coat, not the man. If the party finds his name later (commission papers, log entries, etc.), that's a future thread.
>
> The Knighton who commands the pursuit fleet is [[Rupert-Knighton]]: Commodore, the Knight Squadron, Dravosi Crown Navy. He is also Geoffrey Draves's uncle. The party does not know this. Geoffrey does not yet know Grigori is describing his family. Let that land later, when it will hurt more.
>
> If the party is committed to Tidefall regardless: *"Your ship. Your risk."*

## Threat Threshold

Grigori is not a threat in this scene unless the party creates the conditions. He becomes a problem if they:

1. Refuse to answer the question and won't be moved
2. Threaten him directly, or threaten the crew in front of him
3. Refuse to let any crew off at the next port

In any of those cases: the warmth leaves the room. He does not threaten. He does not move. He says *"That's unfortunate"* and he waits. If they press further, he stands up slowly, and the coat settles around him differently than it did a moment ago, and the room gets very quiet. He still does not speak first.

He can kill everyone at this table without moving from where he stands. He is choosing not to because it would be inconvenient and he finds the dinner pleasant. That calculus changes only if they insist.

> [!DM]
> If combat somehow starts: he is CR 19 with 263 HP, AC 17, CHA 21, and *Mad Monk's Resilience* (CON save to drop to 1 HP vs non-radiant non-crit). He does not die. He does not fall. He thanks them for the meal and walks off the ship. Full stat block: [[content/shattered-sea/situations/active/Shepherd-Grigori\|Shepherd Grigori — DM]].

## After the Scene

Once the party has answered the question and the condition is acknowledged, Grigori glances toward the locked door. Small gesture, barely a nod. The lock turns from the inside and the crew files out: wary, tired, relieved in that specific way of people who had a plan for dying that they no longer need.
