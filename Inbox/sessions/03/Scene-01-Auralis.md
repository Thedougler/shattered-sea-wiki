# Scene 1

> No sensation of falling — you are simply somewhere else.
>
> The cold arrives first. Then the pressure — everywhere at once, the weight of countless fathoms pressing down onto your chest. There is something solid beneath your feet. But the dark around you is unending, the absolute bottom of the world, and your lungs scream for air.
>
> You open your mouth.
>
> Nothing comes. Your chest convulses. Your hands find your throat. There is no up from here. There is no surface. There is only the weight, and the dark, and the stone beneath your feet.

*[Let the player react.]*

> You breathe.
>
> Somehow you breathe.

> [!dm] Auralis pulled Perrin into a real impression of the Drowned Maw, his actual location. The panic is real. Auralis did not prepare him; he does not think the way mortals do.

---

### The Space

![[raw/assets/session-art/session-03-01-auralis-the-space.png|Perrin stands alone on glowing Antheri stone at the bottom of the Drowned Maw while the space itself attends to him]]

> A shape moves at the edges of the dark. Pale things, lit from within, pulsing in long slow rhythms like breath. Dozens of them. Hundreds. Moving in loose constellations through the water around you, unbothered by your presence, casting the only light there is.
>
> Your eyes find it. Adjust to it.
>
> The floor beneath you is worked stone, worn smooth perfectly smooth. In the bioluminescent light you can see channels running through it in long deliberate lines toward the darkness. There are no walls. There is no ceiling. The floor simply continues forever into the black abyss.
>
> The space orients. Something turns its attention toward you — not from a direction you can find, not through anything you can see.

> [!dm] Auralis is not a figure. His attention gives the space its orientation; he attends to Perrin the way a system attends to an anomaly. This is the closest he can get to a conversation.

> [!check] Insight — What is this attention?
> DC 12.
> Crit fail: reads it as malicious — Perrin may react defensively, possibly breaking the contact.
> Fail: nothing readable; the space feels empty, no sense of consciousness.
> Near miss: something is attending, but indistinct.
> Success: monitoring, not predatory. Something flagged Perrin as an anomaly and turned a fraction of itself toward him. This presence is not threatening him. It is not *for* him.
> Crit success: the attending fraction is near-infinitesimal. The rest of Auralis is occupied with something else entirely.

*[Ask Perrin: What does he do? Does he try to speak? Move? Look for an exit?]*

---

### The Hunt

> The bioluminescent things scatter.
>
> The darkness closes in — not the patient deep-ocean dark you have been standing in, but something total, moving, cold against every surface at once. The floor is gone. There is no floor. There is only the water, everywhere, and your lungs empty on reflex and there is nothing to fill them.
>
> Your mouth opens. Closes. Opens.
>
> Nothing.

*[Let the player react.]*

> The breath arrives before you find it. Cold seawater moving through you in a long slow draw, through something you have no word for, somewhere behind the jaw, below the ear. Your chest is not involved. Your chest is enormous. Your body is enormous. It extends behind you in the dark — longer than the Surety, longer than anything you have ever been — and the tail comes last, far back, the long lower lobe trailing through the current, the hook at its end dense and yellowed and scarred white at the joint.
>
> Your skin reads the water in frequencies you cannot name. Temperature. Pressure differentials. Current. The taste of something above — blood, diluted, days old, mixing with storm churn and rain, but there and real and coming from the direction of a hull. The hunger registers it as a direction. The hunger is not yours. It is older than you. It is the oldest thing in this body.
>
> Something else is below.

> [!dm] Ask: *"What does Perrin do with his hands? Are they there?"* The body has no hands. Whatever they describe, let it land and move without comment.

> [!dm] The blood above is from the Surety: Rook fight, Wouters overboard, days at anchor in chummed water. The shark has been circling since before the storm. Auralis isn't hiding it — Perrin can smell it. He is pulling attention downward.

> Below, something breathes.
>
> Not a shape you have a name for. Longer than two ships laid end to end, wider across the back than a warehouse floor, moving through the dark water in slow unhurried arcs. Its breath comes in long cycles — each exhale a warm column rising through the water above it, a deep pulse the body you are in reads as rhythm. As timing. As the exact moment in the cycle when the pectoral fin drifts back and the entry point opens up.
>
> This is not a thought. It is a calculation worn into cartilage over a lifetime of descents. The body already knows the angle.
>
> The body surges.

> [!dm] Just before the surge: *"What does Perrin smell?"* Asking pulls them in even if they have no answer. After the surge: *"What's going through his head?"* Let them answer, nod, and continue. The dream doesn't care.

> The water streams past in the dark. The whale grows below — first a shadow, then a surface, warm and textured and vast. The tail lobe trails and the hook catches the bioluminescent light for one suspended moment.
>
> The impact is a sound you feel in your cartilage. The hook seats. The whale's response is not immediate — the pain reaches you through the connection before it registers anywhere else, the whole body of something that size waking up, the full weight of it deciding to run.
>
> It runs.
>
> The drag is enormous. The tail lobe goes taut. The joint at its base strains under a load that would break anything smaller, the cartilage flexing white and dense under whatever sense this body uses for close focus. The whale dives. Pitches. Sounds into the dark. You go with it — patient, holding, because patience is also in the body.
>
> The surges come. They change. They come less.
>
> And then awareness narrows — by Auralis, by the part of this that is not the hunt — contracts down through the whole length of you to one point: the joint. The base of the tail lobe. White-scarred and flexing under load. You feel it flex. Hold. Flex again.

![[raw/assets/session-art/session-03-01-tail-joint-clue.png|The whale-hunting vision narrows to the whip shark's scarred tail-joint under strain]]

> [!check] Nature (Perrin) — What is this point?
> DC 10; advantage with ship or rigging experience.
> Crit fail: reads it as reinforced, actually strong — misses the vulnerability.
> Fail: a junction, no particular significance.
> Near miss: seam under repeated tension — could matter.
> Success: structural seam where two different tissues meet under load. This seam can fail.
> Crit success (DC 14+): can fail catastrophically under the right force — slashing damage, perpendicular to the grain.

> [!dm] The joint is the structural weak point: slashing damage severs the lobe and frees the ship even if the barb stays. Auralis is showing Perrin the side that can fail. Don't name it. If asked, ask back: *"What does it feel like?"*

> The hunt does not finish.
>
> The ocean leaves.

> The bioluminescent things drift back to the edges of the stone. The floor is beneath your feet. The pressure is still everywhere. The smell of blood from above is still there, still registering, still a direction.
>
> You are the size you have always been.

### The Vision

> The stone beneath your feet goes transparent.
>
> You are looking up through black water at the underside of a ship. Not this ship — older timber, a different hull shape, barnacled and storm-worn. The bioluminescent things scatter. The water goes very still.
>
> From below, from the dark where the light does not reach, something rises.
>
> Fast. Faster than anything that size should move. The lower lobe of its tail streams behind it as it climbs, that long pale whip ending in a hook that catches the bioluminescent light for just a moment before—
>
> The hull above you explodes inward. Timber and copper sheathing and the muffled roar of water rushing into a hole that wasn't there a second ago. The hook is through. The creature drives its weight sideways and the ship above lurches — you can see the whole hull shift — and then it pulls. The barb seats itself deeper with every foot of drag. The ship is going nowhere it does not want it to go.

> [!dm] One of many attacks Auralis has watched from below over centuries. The attack sequence is accurate to the statblock: Hooking Drive drives the barbed tail through the hull, and until the tail is freed the ship cannot move. The people who appear grabbing at the barb are making it worse — pulling on it drives it deeper and grinds the ship toward the shark.

*[Player can react. Let it sit.]*

---

### The Water

> One more vision. Briefer. Someone falling from the stricken ship into the water alongside it — you see them from below, entering the surface from underneath, bubbles and limbs and the white of a face looking down.
>
> The creature turns toward them before they are fully in the water.
>
> The vision ends.

> [!dm] The whip shark has Blood Frenzy (advantage against bloodied creatures) and a Surge reaction (30-ft move + bite when anything enters water within 30 ft). After the Rook fight, anyone overboard is dead before the crew responds. Auralis is showing this, not saying it.

---

### The Withdrawal

> The orientation of the room shifts.
>
> Something below — far below the transparent floor, below the shape, below the channels and the worked stone and whatever the foundation of this place is — pulls the attention of the space away. Not entirely. A fraction of it remains, the way a door left open is still open even when no one is standing in it.
>
> The pale things begin to drift back toward the edges of the stone.
>
> One word arrives. The last one. It costs something — you can feel the cost without knowing what currency is being spent.
>
> **GROW.**

*[Stop. Ask Perrin: does he say it aloud? Does it mean anything to him yet? Let him sit with it before the withdrawal continues.]*

> Then nothing. The space is still here. The stone is still beneath your feet. The pressure is still everywhere. But the orientation is gone. You are alone at the bottom of the world.

> [!dm] Auralis is returning his attention to the fissure; this contact cost focus he could not spare. GROW is not an explanation or a goodbye. It is the one thing he wanted Perrin to carry, and what it means is for later.

---

### The Waking

> The stone goes.
>
> The sea comes back — the smell of it, the spray, the hull groaning under anchor strain in the storm. Your legs have not been holding you. You are already falling, the rail rising past your shoulder, the deck rushing up —

You catch the rail. One hand. One knee on the deck.

The water below the ship is dark.

> [!dm] To every other character, Perrin goes still for a heartbeat at the rail. Don't describe a visible pause unless a PC was already watching closely — even then, just a blink, a hand tightening on wet wood. Cut immediately to the first hard impact.
