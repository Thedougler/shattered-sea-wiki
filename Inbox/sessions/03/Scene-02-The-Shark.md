# The Shark

![[raw/assets/session-art/session-03-02-whip-shark-hooking-drive.png|The whip shark's continuous barbed tail is hooked through the HCS Surety's port hull at the waterline]]

*→ Run guide opener ends at "the lanterns swing hard to port and stay there." Continue here.*

**Starting state:** The shark is circling and probing. The barb has not yet seated. Players can act before the hook lands.

**Target time:** 60–75 minutes. If it runs long, compress aftermath and move to Calveno.

---

## Shark Stat Snapshot

**HP:** 114 | **AC:** 15 | **Attack bonus:** +9 | **Speed:** swim 60 ft.

| Attack | Hit | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Bite | 22 (3d10+6) piercing | Grappled, escape DC 17 |
| Barbed Tail | 17 (2d10+6) slashing | DC 17 Str save or pulled 20 ft; off-vessel means overboard |
| Hooking Drive | Activated on hook seat | Ship cannot move until tail is freed; pull/drown cycle begins |

**Blood Frenzy:** advantage against any creature below max HP.

**Surge:** when a creature enters water within 30 ft., shark moves up to 30 ft. and bites if in range. Not an action. State it clearly once when first relevant.

## Run Loop

Each round:

1. **Name the phase.** Probing, pulling, drowning, or repositioning.
2. **State the ship consequence.** List, hull sound, water, crew movement.
3. **Ask for PC actions.** Let them solve physically.
4. **Use one crew default** if no PC redirects that crew member.
5. **Advance the shark.** Probe strike, hook attempt, pull, or drown.
6. **Remind the table what changed.** Hull worse, breach wider, shark hurt, window open or closed.

Do not track ship HP. Sem is the hull narrator.

## Phase Structure

### Phase 0 — Probing (Rounds 1–2)

![[raw/assets/session-art/session-03-02-probing-fin.png|The whip shark circles off the port beam before the hook seats, visible only as a pale dorsal fin and a wake moving too slowly for its size]]

The shark is testing the hull with glancing tail strikes. A deep underwater percussion from below the port hull, then the hull shaking — like someone swung a beam into it from below. The dorsal fin may break the surface briefly — long, pale, moving too slowly for its size.

Players can act here. Any meaningful intervention (significant damage, deterrent, distraction) can delay or prevent the hook seating. Default: if the party does nothing that matters, the hook seats at the end of Round 2.

**Pre-hook options:**
- Deal 20+ damage during probing → shark recoils; doesn't attempt hook this round
- Chum, bright light, or loud percussion → DC 13 Wis; success delays probe by 1 round. Three successes before it hooks → shark disengages entirely
- Thunk's swivel gun → 2d6; on 8+ the shark recoils and skips its hook attempt this round

### Phase 1 — Hook Seats

One clear beat before the cycle begins:

> The next impact is different. Not a strike — a catch. The hull groans and doesn't stop groaning. The list doesn't correct. From below, Sem starts screaming.

The barb is through the hull, below the waterline, into the hold. The pull begins immediately.

### Phase 2 — The Pull

The shark turns and drags. The **bow swings hard to port** — 30 degrees in a few seconds. The stern whips the other direction. The deck is no longer flat.

> The bow lurches to port like something enormous grabbed it and yanked. The rigging shrieks. Anyone not holding something goes down.

- All PCs and crew on deck: DC 12 Athletics or Acrobatics or fall prone and slide 10 ft. toward port rail
- The shark then **reverses direction** mid-pull — the bow starts to come back, but the hook angle changes, driving the barb deeper

> The pull stops. Then the ship grinds to port again — different direction. The shark is turning. The barb works deeper with every degree.

### Phase 3 — The Drown

The shark pulls down. A whip shark evolved this to drown whales. A wooden ship is not a whale.

> The port rail goes under. The bow tilts hard. Water comes over the side in sheets. Then the ship fights back — she's too light, too full of air. The shark cannot hold her under.

The ship's buoyancy snaps it back to the surface — fast. That snap is half the damage.

> The ship hits the waterline like a dropped plank. The hull rings. Something below breaks. Sem is still screaming but the words are gone.

**Hull condition (Sem narrates — not HP):**
1. *"She can take another one, but not five more."*
2. *"The frames are cracking — she's breathing water now."*
3. *"One more of those and I can't keep her up."*

After stage 3: the ship breaks apart if unresolved.

### Cycle Reset

After the drown, the shark repositions and begins again: probe → hook attempt → pull → drown → repeat. Each full cycle advances Sem's hull narration by one stage.

### Blood Interruption

If blood enters the water (PC overboard, crew blood washing out through the breach, shark wound) — the shark abandons the pull, moves to the blood source, and spends **one round** investigating. This releases hook tension temporarily.

> The tension releases. The ship groans back toward level. Somewhere in the dark water, something is moving toward the blood.

This is the player opportunity window. One round. After it, the shark repositions and re-hooks.

## The Joint

The joint — the scarred seam where the lower lobe begins — is **below the waterline**, outside the hull. It is never visible from the weather deck. Players cannot see it by looking over the rail.

**What they can discover:**
- From the hold: the lobe enters through the breached planking below water level; they can touch it, feel it flexing, probe its thickness — but the joint is further out, outside the hull, underwater
- From above deck: the tail disappears into dark water; there is no visible indication of where the joint sits
- Perrin's dream: "I know where to hit it" — he knows the joint is roughly 10 ft. along the lobe from the hull, and how deep. This matters because without it, players have to guess or go under

**Attacking the joint:**
- Over the rail: reach weapon plunged below waterline. DC 12 Acrobatics first or fall overboard. Attack at disadvantage from unstable deck. 20+ slashing severs the lobe; see DM Triggers.
- Overboard: full access, no disadvantage, but Surge fires immediately. Treat as death pressure.
- Perrin's knowledge removes disadvantage on the over-the-rail attack and lets him direct another PC precisely.

**Cutting the lobe from inside the hold:**
- The lobe passes through the breach into the hold — accessible, but moving and partially submerged
- Needs 3 successful hits (any damage) or 1 hit dealing 15+
- Severs the lobe stub from the shark; barb remains wedged in hull but shark is freed
- Breach widens; Sem screams; shark disengages

> [!dm] The designed solution is the joint (easiest sever mechanic), but cutting the lobe from the hold is a real option with a harder threshold. Any physical plan that makes sense gets a roll and a consequence.

## Ship Navigation During the Fight

What to say when players move through the ship.

**Weather deck (port side):**
Pre-dawn, wet. The list is subtle during probing, violent once hooked. The dorsal fin may break the surface 20–30 ft. off the port beam — long, pale, moving too slowly. The tail disappears into black water at the hull. There is nothing to see below the waterline.

**Companionway down:**
Narrow, listing to port. You feel the impacts through your feet before you hear them. During the pull, you brace against the walls just to get down. After the drown, the bottom step is wet.

**Gun deck:**
The sound is much louder here. The hull flexes between the frames with each impact — you can see it bow inward and spring back. Thunk is at a swivel mount trying to angle it below the waterline, furious.

**Hold (after hook seats):**

![[raw/assets/session-art/session-03-02-hold-lobe.png|Below decks, the whip shark's pale lobe punches through the Surety's lower planking while Sem braces the breach]]

This is where they find the damage. The lobe comes through the lower planking — a thick, pale limb the width of a man's torso, flexing under the tension of the hooked ship. Water weeps around it. With each shark movement, the planking around it splinters outward. The lobe disappears into dark, shin-deep bilge water. You cannot see past the hull breach. The joint is outside, in the water, somewhere below.

## Ship Narration

Use one per round during the probing and hook phases; don't repeat until the fight runs long.

**Probing:**
- A low percussion from below the port hull. Not a wave. Regular.
- The ship rocks to port and doesn't fully settle before the next one comes.
- The rigging shakes for a moment — not wind.
- From below: something moving along the hull, slow and heavy.

**Hooked / pull:**
- The list deepens hard as the shark drives weight into the hull, then partially corrects.
- The rigging goes slack as the bow swings, then snaps taut.
- Salt water comes over the port rail in a sheet.
- Barrels in the hold roll to port and hit the bulkhead together.

**Drown:**
- The port rail goes under. It comes back. Everything not lashed is wet.
- The ship hits the surface hard — not a roll, a strike.
- From below: a sound like a frame cracking, then Sem's voice.

**When the joint severs:**

![[raw/assets/session-art/session-03-02-lobe-release.png|The severed lobe releases with a cannon-shot crack as the Surety rolls back toward level]]

> The sound is a cannon shot. Not a crack — a percussion, something under enormous tension releasing all at once. The shark recoils hard to port. The tail stub wrenches free from the hull with a grinding tear. Water hits the breach fast for three seconds before Sem gets his brace in. The ship rolls back toward level. The port side goes quiet.

## Behavioral Arc

| Shark State | Behavior |
|---|---|
| 114–58 HP | Committed; pressing probing hits; hooking and cycling aggressively |
| 57–29 HP | Testing escape angles; circling wider between cycles; more responsive to deterrents |
| 28 HP or lower | Looking for a reason to disengage; chum, light, pain, or noise can end it |
| 0 HP | It sounds hard and fast. No body. No confirmation. It may be dead; no one knows. |

## After It Ends

The fight ends when the shark disengages, is driven off, or dies.

Immediate aftermath:

- Tail stub or barb remains wedged in hull unless the resolution removed it.
- Sem can keep the ship afloat in calm water, not another storm.
- The *Surety* needs dry dock before rough sailing.
- Move to [[content/shattered-sea/sessions/03/Scene-03-Aftermath|Scene 03 — Aftermath]] quickly.
