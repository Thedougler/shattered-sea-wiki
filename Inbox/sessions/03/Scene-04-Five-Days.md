# At Sea — Five Days

*Run this day by day. Roll flavour, resolve downtime, move to the next day. The Whale is baked into Day 2. Calveno closes Day 5.*

---

## Opening Handoff

*Read once the Surety is clear of the storm and shark:*

> The storm breaks by mid-morning. The sea settles into long grey swells — nothing like last night. Old Faas has lashed the sail. Thunk is bailing. The *Surety* is moving again, slower than she should be, but moving. Five days to Calveno. What do you do?

---

## The Downtime Pitch

*Say this once, at the start of Day 1:*

> "Here's how the next four days work. Eight hours of sleep, eight hours of ship work — watches, rigging, bailing, whatever Sem needs. That leaves eight hours a day that are yours. Five days, one free period each.
>
> The ship's hurt — Sem's already at the hull if you want to put your time toward repair work or learning something from him. Old Faas can teach anyone the charts. The Chart Archive in the captain's cabin is yours if you've got questions about what's waiting in Calveno — harbour approach, city layout, whoever's running what. If someone needs proper rest, Alys has the surgeon's berth; three days of it and she can clear most things out of you. And the crew — if you want to know them, now's the time. If you're a caster and you want something specific in your pocket before we arrive, talk to me about scribing a scroll. And if none of that appeals — tell me what your character would actually do with five days at sea, and we'll figure it out."

## Day 2 — The Whale *(Perrin)*

**Roll d20 → describe the flavour result → then cut to the Whale.**

Someone is on watch. The sea is unusually still.

![[raw/assets/session-art/session-03-04-watch-whale.png|The watch calls Perrin to the bow as a vast bioluminescent whale-shape passes beneath flat grey water]]

> The second morning: no wind, no chop, no roll. The sea has settled flat. The early light is grey. Your breath mists. Somewhere aft, a single rope taps against the mast in air too faint to feel.

> [!check] Perception — First sighting
> DC 10 calm/clear · DC 14 flat water/pre-dawn · DC 18 low visibility
> **Fail:** notices nothing — the creature is fully past; only Perrin sees the last glow.
> **Near miss:** catches the bow wave as it's already passing. Too late for a full view. Say: *"There's a bow wave ahead — and it didn't come from us."*
> **Success:** bow wave, then the creature visible as it passes. Bioluminescence, the slow exhale column.
> **Crit success:** full view from first movement — outline, glow, the long breath.

> [!check] Nature — What is it?
> DC 12 species recognition · DC 16 full identification
> **Fail:** calls it a sea monster. May alarm the crew before it's past.
> **Near miss:** breathing cycle marks it non-predatory. Nothing more.
> **Success:** real species, not a monster. Bioluminescence and size confirm it.
> **Crit success (DC 16+):** same species as the thing in Perrin's Auralis dream. Give Perrin advantage — if he hits DC 16, he recognises the shape he was inside.

*Ask Perrin: Does he say anything? Does he tell whoever's on watch what he knows?*

> [!dm] Same species as the dream. Perrin knows that shape — he hunted one. Nobody else has a name for it. Don't explain. Let Perrin decide what to say and to whom.
> If Perrin is not on watch: call him up. *"Black-Jaw. Come look at this."* The whale is still visible when he arrives.
> The GROW seed: he was inside something that hunts this. He is very small.

**Spotlight:** Perrin. Secondary: whoever else is on watch.

*Then go around: what does each PC do with their free period today? Resolve using [Quick-Run: Activities](#quick-run-activities).*

## Central Strait Flavour — d20

| d20 | What they see |
|---|---|
| 1 | A school of flying fish erupts ahead of the bow — hundreds of silver bodies arcing in the morning light for thirty seconds, then gone. |
| 2 | Merchant traffic on the horizon all morning — sails heading west, then east, then west again. The Strait is busy here. |
| 3 | The water turns pale and milky for two miles — off-white, too shallow-looking for open sea. You lean over the rail and find it: jellyfish, thousands of them, translucent blue-white, drifting just beneath the surface. |
| 4 | The water changes colour for an hour — deeper blue-green, noticeably colder. Old Faas calls it a current tongue running up from the Maw. He doesn't elaborate. |
| 5 | A pair of Crown frigates heading east in formation, running with the wind. They're moving fast. Noor watches them through the glass until they're gone. |
| 6 | A pod of dolphins rides the bow wave for the better part of an hour, and then they're gone as suddenly as they appeared. |
| 7 | A Tessarine merchant brig passes close to starboard, heading north under full sail. She's heavy-laden — low in the water. Trade goods moving toward the Crown Islands. |
| 8 | The sky at dusk goes amber-green, layered low on the horizon. Old Faas says it means calm for two more days, in a tone that suggests calm is not always good. |
| 9 | A sea turtle surfaces close to the hull — huge, barnacled, moving with total indifference to the ship. Old Faas says they've been sailing these routes since before the Dravosi built their first pier. |
| 10 | A mat of kelp the size of the ship drifts north, moving against the surface current. Old Faas says that happens this time of year — something about the deep current running the other way. |
| 11 | A pair of Aarakocra pass high overhead, moving south in a straight line without circling. Courier flight, Old Faas says. Someone needs word of something, and fast. |
| 12 | Bioluminescent plankton in the night wake — the ship leaves a river of cold blue light behind her for three hours. Anyone who puts a hand in the water sees their fingers glow. |
| 13 | A Grung fishing raft — not a canoe, a proper raft big enough for six — passes half a mile off the port bow, net out, catch looking good. Normal as anything. |
| 14 | The wind dies completely for four hours. The Surety sits becalmed under a sky the colour of old pewter. Then the wind returns from a different direction entirely. |
| 15 | Seabirds, thousands of them, resting on the water in every direction — you sail through them for an hour. They move just enough to clear the hull, then settle back. |
| 16 | In the early morning, very far away, the sound of something large exhaling. Not the Surety, not the crew. Something that lives here. Old Faas doesn't look up from his charts. |
| 17 | A Rattkin trader — small, quick, riding high — cuts across the Surety's course half a mile ahead, flying no faction pennant. Old Faas says that's normal east of Midchain. |
| 18 | The stars are unusually clear tonight. Noor can name most of them. She says navigators of the Tail use completely different names for the same sky — different stories for the same lights. |
| 19 | A two-hour rain squall with no wind. The sea goes flat and grey; visibility drops to fifty feet. The crew works in silence. Then it clears as if nothing happened. |

## Quick-Run: Activities

### Recover

Alys supervises. She brings what they need from the surgeon's cabinet.

- **Condition / disease / poison:** DC 15 Con save, advantage with Alys present. Success clears it.
- **Exhaustion:** No roll. 3 full days of rest removes 1 level.

*Read-aloud (optional):*
> Alys checks on you twice a day. She doesn't say much. On the third morning she declares you fit — which in her vocabulary is high praise.

---

### Train with Crew

One crew member per proficiency. Track days cumulatively toward 250 total.

| Crew | Teaches |
|---|---|
| Sem Holst | Carpenter's Tools |
| Old Faas | Navigator's Tools, Vehicles (Water) |
| Thunk | Smith's Tools |
| Alys Kuiper | Herbalism Kit |
| Noor | Navigator's Tools — theory only; she's still learning herself |
| Geoffrey Draves | Vehicles (Water) |
| Grigori | One scholarly language, DM's pick |

> [!dm] Tell them honestly: 5 days is 2% of the way. Only worth tracking if a player has a specific goal they're already committed to.

---

### Research

Player states one specific question. Pick the best crew source and set DC. Roll Intelligence (History) or Wisdom (Perception) depending on the question — or just give them the answer if the source is willing and the question is direct.

| Question | Source | DC |
|---|---|---|
| Calveno harbour / La Vasca dry dock capacity | Old Faas | 12 |
| Calveno districts / city layout | Old Faas, Geoffrey | 14 |
| Crown patrol procedures | Geoffrey Draves | 12 |
| Dravosi presence in Calveno | Geoffrey Draves | 14 |
| Cannon operation / ship armaments | Thunk | 10 |
| Field medicine / herbalism | Alys | 12 |
| Religious or scholarly lore | Grigori | 10 — he gives freely |
| Noble court politics, Shattered Sea-wide | Grigori | 8 — he knows everyone |
| Central Strait weather and hazards | Old Faas | 10 |

> [!dm] **Grigori as a research source:** He answers without hesitation. His information is accurate and detailed — he has met half the ruling families of the Shattered Sea. For every two questions he answers, he asks one back. He is actually curious.

---

### Spend Time with Crew

One crew member per day. No roll — this is about what the player wants from the scene.

> [!dm] **Grigori:** Play the warmth as real. He is genuinely curious about the party. He asks about history, fears, goals — naturally, in conversation. One Grigori detail per scene: he never sleeps (if it's late), his hands are always cold (if they shake hands), or he's praying alone at 3am in a language that isn't Common (if they catch him mid-ritual). He does not explain. He is not rattled if asked.

| Crew | What they'll talk about | What surfaces |
|---|---|---|
| **Sem Holst** | The hull damage — he's worried about it | La Vasca can patch the planking; the figurehead mount is gone entirely |
| **Old Faas** | Prefers to teach over talk | If pressed: he knew someone who ran cargo for Rook once. Four of them came back |
| **Thunk** | The Crown Shipyards, the guns, the work he misses | He's been teaching himself to bake. The galley is the only quiet place on the ship |
| **Alys Kuiper** | Medicine; nothing personal | She served on a Crown frigate. She left before the sentence ends |
| **Noor** | Navigation theory; she wants to learn, not teach | She's mapping this route herself, in a private journal she doesn't show anyone |
| **Geoffrey Draves** | Tidefall, the Crown, the life he had | Unprompted: *"I don't know what I'm loyal to anymore."* Then changes the subject |
