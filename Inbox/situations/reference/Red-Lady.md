# The *[[Red-Lady|Red Lady]]*

> [!READ-ALOUD]
> A two-masted brigantine rides low in the water, her hull stained a deep, even red from waterline to rail. Seven cannon ports line each side beneath the gun deck, and swivel mounts sit at bow and stern. At the prow, a pale wooden figurehead of a cloaked woman leans forward with one hand open at her side. The ship is long, clean-lined, and built with the hard, practical look of a vessel meant to chase, fight, and keep going.

## Current State

The *Red Lady*, the flagship of [[Fisks-Fleet]], sits at the Maw's eastern edge, where the continental shelf begins its descent toward the trench floor. The four other ships of the fleet — *[[content/shattered-sea/situations/reference/Narrow|The Narrow]]*, *[[content/shattered-sea/situations/reference/Heft]]*, *[[content/shattered-sea/situations/reference/Fernen]]*, and *[[content/shattered-sea/situations/reference/Loud-Argument|Loud Argument]]* — went down at the same time and lie nearby. The [[content/shattered-sea/items/Pearl-of-Souls]] sank with the *Red Lady* and still lies at the wreck site.

## Overview

A two-masted brigantine, 96 feet on the waterline, privateer-rigged. The hull ran dark red from waterline to rail — not painted, stained deep into the wood, uniform and deliberate. The figurehead was a cloaked woman carved in pale wood, eyes forward, one hand open at her side. Two gun decks, seven cannon ports per side, swivel mounts fore and aft. She rode low and purposeful, built long for her beam.

Built for extended independent operations: fast enough to disengage, armed enough to press, fitted out for months without port resupply. She was in prime condition when she sank. No deferred maintenance. Fully crewed and armed.

## The Wreck

[[Umberlee-DM|Umberlee]]'s storm came as fog first — so thick the water was invisible from the deck, surrounding all five ships simultaneously. Moments later a rogue trench opened in the fleet's path, plunging all five ships 80 feet down into a valley of water. The walls closed in and swallowed the fleet whole. [[Crissdalynn-Khinriss]] dove from the crow's nest, grabbed [[content/shattered-sea/private/players/Delmar-Fisk|the Admiral]], and flew straight back up — she had only risen 50 feet when the water closed in; momentum and buoyancy carried them both to the surface. They heard [[Umberlee-DM|Umberlee]]'s laughter on the wind, then her rage, as the ships went under. The *Red Lady* went down with the [[content/shattered-sea/items/Pearl-of-Souls]] still aboard.

## The Aftermath

All hands lost in the wreck — souls collected by the [[content/shattered-sea/items/Pearl-of-Souls]] at the wreck site. The five captains' souls were pressed into [[content/shattered-sea/private/players/Delmar-Fisk]] to hold them for later. See [[Fisks-Fleet]] for the full captain roster.

## Layout

The *Red Lady* is laid out so the crew can live aboard for long stretches without wasting movement. The back of the ship is for officers and command, the middle is for guns and daily work, and the deepest decks carry the weight, heat, food, powder, and locked stores that keep the whole ship running.

### Weather Deck

The weather deck is mostly open, with just enough built structure at the back to mark rank. On the raised afterdeck sit the wheel, compass stand, speaking grates to the deck below, and the **Signal Station**, where flags, lamps, signal drums, and code books are kept ready. Beneath the stern windows is the stern cabin block: the **Captain's Quarters** on the starboard side with bunk, desk, lockbox, and sea chest; the **Fleet Intelligence Room** at the back center with shelves, map drawers, cipher files, and a bolted table for private briefings. Forward of the cabin block the deck opens into working space around the mainmast, hatch gratings, line pins, pumps, longboat tackle, and the lanes crews need to move powder, shot, and wounded men. At the bow are anchor gear, cable lockers, and the forward swivel mount; at the stern rail is the aft swivel.

### Cannon Deck

The cannon deck is the ship's loud, crowded heart. It runs nearly the full length of the hull with seven cannon ports on each side, broadside guns chained in place, and a central passage left clear for runners, powder carriers, and damage control. At the stern is the officers' end of the deck: the **Navigator's Station** in a chart room under the afterdeck, with shelves of rolled charts, route logs, dividers, spare lanterns, and a [[Narrow]] table strapped for use in rough water. Nearby are the first mate's berth, a small officers' mess, and a stern ladder up to the captain. Midships are the main hatch, rope lockers, sail repair benches, tool chests, and racks for rammers, swabs, and spare gun tackle. Toward the bow the crew space becomes more communal: hanging hammocks, sea chests under the beams, a mess table that can be cleared fast, the bosun's locker, and the heads screened off near the bow where the stink can be thrown forward into the wind.

### Lower Belowdecks

The next deck down is tighter, darker, and more private, where the ship's long-voyage life is stored and patched back together. Near the middle sits the **Surgeon's Berth**, with a braced operating table, medicine chest, washbasin, straps, shelves of bandages and spirits, and just enough curtained space for two or three wounded men to recover out of the way. Around it are the practical rooms that make the ship feel inhabited: a lamp room for protected flame, sailcloth and rope lockers, spare hammock netting, slop storage, carpenter's stores, water casks, and bins for salted food not needed in the galley that day. There is also a small marines' ready room for boarding kit, shields, and hooks kept close to hand. Passage space is deliberately kept clear from the ladders to the surgeon and aft toward the powder access.

### Hold

The hold is where the ship stops feeling like a workplace and starts feeling like a machine built around weight. In the back of the hold, behind locked doors and strict lantern rules, is the powder magazine beside the **Weapons Locker**, where boarding weapons, spare muskets, pistols, axes, shot, match, and reserve powder tools are kept under guard. Slightly forward of midships is the **Cook's Galley**, a cramped brick-lined cooking space beside the fresh-water cistern, with hanging utensils, dry stores, barrels of grain, hooks for smoked meat, and enough room for one cook and one helper to work without killing each other. The rest of the hold is ballast, cargo lashings, spare timber, chain, tar, bilge access, and low-ceilinged crawlspace where sailors curse, sweat, and look for leaks by lantern light.
