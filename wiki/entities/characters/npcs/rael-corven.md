## Overview

Rael captained the *[[red-lady|Red Lady]]*, the flagship. Sorcerer. He was [[delmar-fisk|Fisk]]'s flag captain — the closest to the Admiral operationally of the five, and the one who had been closest to Fisk long before either of them commanded anything.

They met on the [[midchain|Midchain]] as young crewmen on another man's vessel. Fisk read people; Rael read conditions. Between the two of them, very little got missed.

He was the only captain who entered [[umberlee|Umberlee]]'s shrine alongside Fisk, navigating to the Pearl using Detect Magic. He was standing next to Fisk when it ended.

He drowned in the Maw when Umberlee closed the water over all five ships. His soul was pressed into Delmar at the moment of sinking. Of the five captains now inside Fisk, Rael's voice is the loudest — he was there, he knows exactly what they took, and he was the last one Fisk saw before the water came in.

## Appearance & Manner

Sea-worn and solid, close-cropped dark hair going iron-grey at the temples. His clothes are plain and properly fitted. The face offers little: a jaw set somewhere between resolve and fatigue, eyes that have stopped performing patience. His hands are the only betrayal — a sorcerer's hands, faintly luminous at the knuckles.

**Voice:** Speaks rarely. Everything lands like a ruling.

## Backstory

His father was a merchant sailor, a precise and careful man who ran his life by a single philosophy: *prepare for the worst, work toward the best*. The navy sank his ship on false grounds and classified the cargo as pirate salvage. Rael learned, eventually, what had happened — and what had been taken along with his father's life.

He has not forgiven the Navy since. He has also never bothered to distinguish between the specific officers responsible and the institution they represent.

His father's philosophy survived him. Rael absorbed it young: training rotations, victualling plans, the way he kept the magazine stocked against contingencies he hoped never to meet. The *Red Lady* ran the way it ran because Rael treated preparation as a moral position.
