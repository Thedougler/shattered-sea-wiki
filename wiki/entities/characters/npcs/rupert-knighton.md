## Overview

Rupert Knighton commands a fleet. Not patrol cutters and manifest stamps — a fleet, Crown naval vessels, the kind that doesn't arrive for inspections. When his ships appear on the horizon, someone has already made a serious mistake.

He is not a privateer. He is not enforcement. He is the thing enforcement reports to when enforcement fails.

Within the Dravosi Crown apparatus, [[barnaby-rook|Barnaby Rook]] was a working piece on the board, not a peer. Rook's death alone might not reach him. [[capn-gorgeous|Cap'n Gorgeous]] was his son — or close enough to House Knighton to amount to the same. The party killed him in Session 01. Knighton doesn't know yet. When he hears, his response will not go through Crown channels.

---

## The Knights

Knighton refers to every ship under his command as one of his Knights — not by their registered hull names, which are administrative. His fleet is his chessboard. How many ships that is, exactly, is not public knowledge. Enough that a port captain who hears "the Knights are coming" doesn't ask follow-up questions.

## Appearance

Older, tall, and broad — a commander's frame that fills a doorway. Slicked-back dark hair greying at the temples, a thick dark mustache, and a black eyepatch over one eye. Wears an immaculate, crease-free Crown-grey Age-of-Sail naval frock coat with gold facings, a high collar, and a white cravat. Composed menace under a genial surface; authority carried in stillness, not posture. *(First rendered in [[session-06-run-guide|Session 06]] — the "King Bradley" register.)*

## Manner

Posh in the old way — the kind that doesn't need to announce itself. He doesn't shout. He doesn't threaten. He gives orders, and the orders happen. His reputation is built on outcomes, not style. He is fearsome precisely because he is not theatrical about it.

> *"He doesn't threaten. He replaces."* — Crown officers who've seen him work.

## Tactics

Every commander in the Dravosi Navy charges. Every commander flanks. Knighton does both, in sequence, in ways that make the enemy feel stupid afterward.

His signature is commitment: he commits his vanguard hard — harder than makes sense, harder than anyone believes is intentional — and the opposing line compresses to meet it. Then the flank arrives. Not a feint. Not a probe. The real weight, from a direction that was open thirty seconds ago and somehow isn't anymore.

Other fleet commanders study his engagements and come away with notes they can't use. The moves are not complicated. They simply require a level of coordinated nerve that most officers can't sustain under fire. He doesn't win every battle. He wins the ones he chooses to fight.

## Connection to Geoffrey

[[geoffrey-draves|Geoffrey Draves]] is his nephew by blood. Knighton almost certainly does not think about this. Geoffrey has never met him. The Draves branch is not the Knighton branch — it's the branch that didn't go anywhere in particular.

Whether Knighton would extend consideration to a nephew who has defected to work for a foreign crew is a question that hasn't come up yet.
