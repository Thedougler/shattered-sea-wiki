*Common item. No attunement required. Non-magical.*

Volume II of [[algernon-reginald-clyde|A.R. Clyde]]'s multi-volume survey of oceanic fauna. Covers appearance, behaviour, habitat, and practical identification for maritime creatures. The author's contempt for his subject matter is evident throughout and is, by reader consensus, an improvement over other texts in the genre.

[[perrin-black-jaw|Perrin Black-Jaw]] carries Volume II. The spine is cracked and the margins are annotated in his hand — Ch. 13 in particular.

Consulting a relevant chapter requires 10 minutes of reading.

## Excerpts

### Ch. 6 — [[sawek|The Sawek]]

> *Look into the suffocating abyss of the blue hole and you will find a creature of sheer, unadulterated madness. The locals call it the **Sawek**, a terrifying compromise of evolution that folds its impossible anatomy into deceptively small caves.*
>
> *The front half is the blunt, soulless engine of a shark, driven only by cold, mechanical necessity, while the rear erupts into a chaos of twenty-foot, ink-stained tentacles that reach out from the dark like the pale fingers of a drowned corpse. It does not pursue; it waits with the terrifying patience of an ambush predator, snatching any man foolish enough to swim past.*
>
> *The divers leave only a pathetic piece of string staked near its lair to warn others — a fragile, heartbreaking gesture staked against the murderous, unfeeling indifference of the ocean.*

### Ch. 7 — [[whip-shark|The Whip-Shark]]

> *Look at the churning currents of the [[shattered-sea|Shattered Sea]]. Here, evolution is not a benevolent designer, but a blind, raving lunatic that has forged the Welak — or the **Whip-Shark**, as naive sailors call it. It is a terrifying monument to destruction, reaching fifty-five feet in length. Thirty-five feet of this is a torpedo of cartilaginous muscle built for the punishing void, while the remaining twenty feet tapers into a dense whip terminating in a recurved, bone-shattering sickle.*
>
> *When it hunts, it executes a calculated atrocity. It rakes its immense scythe across the hulls of our fragile vessels with the mechanical precision of an industrial press, dragging crippled ships and screaming men down into the abyss. This beast possesses a profound, predatory intellect; it knows the ship is merely a floating tin of meat.*

### Ch. 9 — [[ridgeback|The Ridgeback]] *(Naitaka pelagica, provisional)*

> *I have endeavoured, throughout this volume, to approach each specimen with the rigour that science demands and the contempt that direct observation inevitably produces. The creature I now describe I have not observed directly. I have observed, instead, four survivors of the packet vessel* Pale Commission*, and I found the experience considerably less comfortable.*
>
> *The **Ridgeback** — sailors' name; I have retained it for want of a better one — is, by all available account, a serpentine creature of extraordinary scale. The identifying feature is what the sailors call "the humps": three arching sections of the creature's dorsal surface breaching in sequence as it moves, at full depth, toward its quarry. The witnesses agree on the spacing — perhaps thirty feet between each arch — which, if accurate, implies a creature of no fewer than ninety feet in overall length.*
>
> *It is black. Not the dark blue-black of deep water, not the mottled grey of your common large predator. Black in the manner of a thing from which light has been permanently excused. Survivors describe looking at it at close range and seeing nothing — not detail, not surface, not texture. An absence that moves.*
>
> *It does not ambush. This is the detail I find most troubling. The humps are not a warning. They are simply what it looks like when it moves.*
>
> *What I am prepared to record is this: the ocean appears to have produced something that accepts payment. A deckhand, throwing overboard his coins, his boots, a piece of brass fitting — the creature released its hold and left. I find this profoundly, personally offensive. Give the thing what it wants. I hate to write it. But there it is.*

### Ch. 10 — [[krakling|The Krakling]] *(Octopoda noctis, provisional)*

> *The **Krakling** — a word that arrived in the Kalowe dockside vocabulary approximately three weeks ago — is an eight-armed cephalopod of approximately ten feet across the mantle, with arms extending a further twelve to fifteen feet in each direction and a beak at the centre of its face mass that is, by the eyewitness descriptions I have collected, structurally identical to that of a parrot.*
>
> *It has no eyes. The face mass is a disc of flat, expressionless skin with the beak at its centre. Nothing else. Evolution has produced a predator that hunts entirely by feel, in the dark, and has found this arrangement so satisfactory that it dispensed with vision entirely.*
>
> *The approach is the part I find most disagreeable. The ship is simply a ceiling that has something interesting on the other side of it, and the creature positions itself beneath the keel with a patience that every survivor described, unprompted, as feeling deliberate. The first sign is the arm-tips. Eight of them, appearing one by one around the hull at the waterline — breaking the surface in sequence, feeling the planking, testing the rail. By the time the crew has identified what is happening, three of them have been grabbed.*
>
> *The good news is that the creature will retreat if sufficiently damaged. The bad news is that anything it was still holding when it went under, it has taken with it.*

### Ch. 13 — [[leviathan|Leviathan]]

> *The true terror of this titan is not merely its staggering immensity, but the agonizing omen of its arrival. Long before it breaks the surface, the sea itself begins to scream in protest. A profound thermal distortion rises from the depths — an unnatural, boiling aura that turns the ocean into a steaming cauldron within sixty feet of its mass. The water bubbles and writhes, cooking everything in its path.*

> [!dm]
> Perrin's copy has heavy annotations in the margins of Ch. 13 (Leviathan). The Vestra was destroyed by something Perrin survived; these margin notes may reveal how much he knows or suspects about what sank his ship.
