# Magister's Monocle

*Wondrous item · Uncommon · No attunement required*

A brass-rimmed single lens on a retractable chain, ground from pale green sea-glass and edged with a thin filing of iron taken — per Calveno tradition — from the chain that hangs outside every magistrate's court. The lens has a slight blue-green cast that makes everything through it look like it's been held underwater.

Calveno magistrates used these to review contract documents for forgery. The underworld figured out the other use within a generation.

---

## Properties

### Read the Mark

If you spend at least **1 minute** observing a creature through the monocle — watching them walk, talk, gesture, negotiate — you build a read on them. This grants:

- **Advantage on your next Insight check** against that creature (used or wasted at the end of the next long rest).
- **One free Sneak Attack die** on the first attack you make against that creature in the subsequent 24 hours, regardless of whether Sneak Attack conditions are otherwise met. The die is 1d6 and does not stack with existing Sneak Attack.

The monocle reads one creature at a time. Building a new read displaces the previous one.

### Catch the Lie

While wearing the monocle and looking at a creature that is actively speaking, you have advantage on Insight checks to determine if they are lying. This requires concentration on the conversation — you cannot be in combat.

### Counter-Tell

Once per long rest, when a creature you have **read** attempts a Deception check against you, you can use your **reaction** to impose disadvantage on the roll (you notice the tell before the words are out).

---

## Limitations

- The monocle must be worn — held to the eye counts only for the Read the Mark observation period, not ongoing passive use.
- Creatures without readable tells — constructs, undead, and creatures under magical compulsion to believe their own lies — are immune to Catch the Lie and Counter-Tell. The free Sneak Attack die still applies.
- The 1-minute observation period must be uninterrupted and at a distance where you can see the subject's face or body language clearly (within 30 ft.).

## History

The oldest Calveno guild contracts specify that a licensed magistrate must review key clauses wearing a *lente del giudice* — a "judge's lens" — to certify no forgery. The specification dates to the Dravosi annexation period, when the Crown's notaries were widely suspected of altering originals after signing.

The sea-glass grinding technique is a Calveno craft tradition. Genuine magistrate's monocles are stamped with the Mercatura's iron-ring seal on the rim. This one is not.

It passed through at least two hands in the Calveno underworld before ending up as part of a confiscated smuggler's inventory, sitting in a harbor authority storage room until someone broke the lock. It moves in the same markets as forged letters of passage — discreetly, for people who deal in leverage.
