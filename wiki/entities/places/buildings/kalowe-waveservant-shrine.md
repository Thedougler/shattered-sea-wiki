# Waveservant Shrine — Kalowe

## Overview

The Waveservant Shrine is [[umberlee|Umberlee]]'s causeway shrine at [[kalowe|Kalowe]], cut into the limestone at the main causeway junction where ships passing through the reef gap are visible from the doorway.

## Description

A stone niche in the causeway wall, covered by a low roof of salvaged timber and sealed tile. Inside: a limestone basin, a shallow ring of tallow candles, and a single [[waveservants|Waveservant]] in salt-grey robes who has been watching ships come through the reef gap long enough that the work has become automatic. The basin drains through a crack in the causeway stone into the lagoon. Nobody sealed it. It doesn't need sealing.

Kalowe does not have a culture of ceremony and the shrine does not pretend otherwise. You step in, you pay, you receive an acknowledgement, you leave. If you want a blessing spoken aloud, that is available. Most captains who work Kalowe regularly don't bother asking.

The gap in the reef is visible from the shrine doorway on clear mornings. That view has done more for tribute compliance than any posted doctrine.

## Function

Mariners pay before they sail or after they survive. Both are acceptable. Neither is optional. A single [[waveservants|Waveservant]] handles everything — collections, candles, blessings, the brief ledger notation.

## Current Hooks

A ship whose captain skipped tribute coming in through the reef gap will be noted. Kalowe's authority is informal, but the shrines have long memories and short ledgers.
