# The Central Strait

![[Central-Strait.webp|Wide blue-green shipping strait cutting between low islands and reef shallows]]

## Overview

The Central Strait is the main east-west highway of the Scatter — a broad corridor of pale blue-green water running forty to eighty miles wide between the [[crown-islands|Crown Islands]] to the north and the [[midchain|Midchain]] to the south. The water is clear enough that the sandy bottom shows through on good days, though the sounding line will tell you it is eighty to two hundred feet down and the bottom has no opinions about your draft. The Scatter Current pushes steadily east through the whole length of it, giving eastbound ships half a knot to a full knot they did not have to earn. Pods of spinner dolphins work the shipping lanes year-round, and flying fish break silver off every bow from the western mouth to the Narrows.

Deep water, sheltered approaches, and a current behind you: three things that rarely arrive together. Almost everything moving between the colonial homelands and [[midchain|Midchain]] uses the Strait. The cost of that convenience is paid at the western end, where [[port-tidefall|Port Tidefall]] extends its inspection pier across the channel and nothing enters under Crown law without pausing to answer questions and sign things.

Eastbound, the Strait narrows as both arcs converge toward the Tail. The current accelerates. The water changes character. By the time Stenmark's cliffs are visible to the north, the comfortable certainties of the mid-Strait are already behind you.

## Geography

At its widest — roughly south of Harwick — the Strait runs close to eighty miles across. At the western mouth it compresses to forty miles between [[calders-tooth|Calder's Tooth]] and the nearest Midchain approaches. At the eastern end, where the arcs pinch toward the Tail, it narrows further and the sounding depths increase sharply as the shelf gives way to the deep water that eventually becomes the Drowned Maw.

The prevailing winds blow northeast to southwest across the whole Scatter. Eastbound ships run with them and the current both; the Strait is generous in that direction. Westbound ships beat against the wind for the full length of the passage, tacking between the northern and southern shore, which is how experienced pilots develop opinions about which tacks are less bad. The broad mid-Strait is the worst of it. Closer to the Midchain's northern reef faces or the Crown Islands' southern coasts there are lee pockets worth knowing.

Fog forms in the eastern Strait overnight and burns off by mid-morning, more reliably in summer than in winter. The western mouth stays clearer. Rain squalls move through fast and without much warning; the spinner dolphins tend to sound before them, which is the kind of navigation aid that does not appear on any chart.

## The Scatter Current

The Scatter Current is the defining fact of Strait navigation. It runs east through the full length of the passage, strongest in mid-channel and in the deeper sections of the Blue Lane, weakest close to the northern and southern reef approaches where bottom friction slows it. A ship crossing the current at the wrong angle loses way noticeably. Every experienced captain knows where the current runs hard and plans accordingly.

The current turns north at the eastern end, bending away from the Strait and up along the Maw's western edge rather than running out into open water. Eastbound ships that miss the turn ride it north instead of through — a navigational error with consequences ranging from inconvenient to serious depending on how close they are to Stenmark's cliffs when they realise it. That bend in the current is called the Cold Turn by pilots who have run it and the Fool's Assist by pilots who have watched others miss it.

## Routes & Access

**The Tidefall Gate** — The western entrance proper: the navigable water between [[calders-tooth|Calder's Tooth]]'s eastern shore and the northernmost Midchain reefs. Forty miles across on a clear day with the Crown's inspection pier visible from the south approach. Entering the Gate means entering Crown jurisdiction. Ships that try to skirt the northern edge and slip past Tidefall without presenting papers generally find that the Crown patrols know exactly which reef gap they are using and have formed an opinion about it.

**The Blue Lane** — The established mid-Strait shipping route, following the strongest line of the Scatter Current roughly equidistant between the northern and southern shores. It is not a marked channel. It is where every merchant goes because that is where every merchant went before them. The water is deepest here, the current is strongest, and a ship in the Blue Lane is visible to both shores.

**The Calven Roads** — The broad, sheltered water south of Calven island, between the Calven coast and the mid-Strait. The prevailing wind is partially blocked by the island mass, making this one of the better anchorage options in the northern Strait for ships waiting out weather or tide. [[calveno|Calveno]]'s canal city is visible from here on clear days.

**The Harwick Reach** — The deeper water south of Harwick, where the island's deep-water anchorage faces onto the Strait. Crown patrol frigates stage from here. A ship on the Blue Lane can see the masts of the Harwick Naval Yard from mid-channel in calm conditions.

**Sandtable Shoal** — A mid-Strait sandbank south of [[aldenmere|Aldenmere]], where the bottom rises from two hundred feet to less than forty over roughly two miles before dropping back again. It is charted and well-marked. The shoal deflects the Blue Lane slightly south before it recovers its line. The Crown salvage contract for Sandtable groundings is currently held by a [[kalowe|Kalowe]] operator, which the Harwick Admiralty considers an embarrassment.

**The Kalowe Run** — The angled approach from the Blue Lane south to [[kalowe|Kalowe]], cutting through the northern Midchain reef approaches. Not a formal channel; agreed waypoints passed pilot to pilot rather than chart to chart.

**The Narrows** — The eastern compression of the Strait, where both arcs close in and the corridor width drops to under twenty miles. The current runs fast here. Fog hangs later in the morning. The water changes colour as the Drowned Maw's influence begins — not dramatic discolouration, but something subtler: the blue-green going a shade deeper, the bottom no longer visible even on clear days.

## Landmarks

**Fort Crestwall** — [[fort-crestwall|The fortified Crown post]] above [[port-tidefall|Port Tidefall]], visible from the western approach and south into the Strait on clear days.

**The Pale City** — [[calveno|Calveno]] visible from mid-Strait: pale stone facades catching morning light, the city's height above the waterline making it legible at distances where most settlements are invisible.

**The Yard Masts** — The standing masts and shipway cranes of the Harwick Naval Yard, visible from mid-channel when the weather cooperates.

**The Eyrie Light** — The [[high-eyrie|High Eyrie]] sea stack visible from the eastern Strait on clear days: a dark vertical mark on the northeastern horizon. Spotting it means you are positioned correctly to avoid the worst of the Spillback. Pilots call it the Eyrie Light not because it carries a light but because it confirms position.

**Stenmark Tower** — The Crown signal station on Stenmark's eastern cliff edge is the last charted landmark before the Narrows close fully. A signal flag on the tower means the Drowned Maw is behaving unusually.

## Hazards

Reef sharks work the shallows and reef approaches on both shores. Hunter sharks range through the deeper lanes, particularly along the Harwick Reach. Giant octopuses occupy wreckage around Sandtable Shoal and the Spillback limestone.

The [[sawek|Sawek]] is the Strait's rumoured apex — a blue-hole predator associated with the darkest sections of the southern Midchain approaches. Its range in the open Strait is unclear. Pilots do not treat deep, still water in the Strait as empty water.

## People & Power

The Strait is nominally Crown-controlled because the western gate, inspection regime, convoy practice, and northern harbours all favour Dravosi administration. In practice, the Strait belongs to whoever can read the current, afford the papers, pick the right pilot, and avoid being noticed at the wrong time. [[kalowe|Kalowe]] operators, Midchain smugglers, Crown patrols, Tidefall officials, and Harwick naval interests all shape passage.

> [!dm]
> Less a sea lane than a managed funnel — Dravosi inspection at Tidefall is a tax and intelligence chokepoint, not just customs. Smuggling success depends on timing, paperwork quality, and who was paid at the western mouth before departure. Raise pressure through inspections, delays, seized manifests. Use weather windows as economic leverage. Convoy timing is political information.

## Piracy

Piracy in the Strait usually arrives wearing paperwork before it raises a flag. Small crews work the Tidefall Gate approaches, where ships have slowed for inspection. The [[pirate-captain|captain]] of the [[the-bad-receipt|Bad Receipt]] is the name merchants lower their voices for along the southern approach: a Kalowe-refit schooner with enough false registry to make a lawful captain hesitate. If the Glass Debt or Velvet Noose is reported anywhere near the Strait, convoys consolidate and Crown patrol schedules stop pretending to be normal.

## Trade & Resources

Deep water, sheltered approaches, and the east-running Scatter Current make the Strait the main trade artery of the region. It carries colonial cargo, inspected shipping, free-port traffic, salvage contracts, convoy escorts, Tidefall fees, and enough fishing activity to feed predators along both shores.
