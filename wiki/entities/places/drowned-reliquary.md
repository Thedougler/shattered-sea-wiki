# The Drowned Reliquary

> [!read-aloud]
> The water here does not smell like the canal. It smells older — salt and stone and something faintly organic, like kelp in a sealed room. The canal wall behind the fish market has a crack at the waterline that is not a crack. It is a doorway, and it has been a doorway for a very long time.

A submerged shrine beneath the harbour district of [[calveno|Calveno]], in the oldest structural stratum of the city — built before Calveno was Calveno, before [[umberlee|Umberlee]] claimed these waters, when something else was worshipped below the tide line. The Waveservants know it exists and leave it alone. Smugglers operating out of [[the-velvet-noose|The Velvet Noose]] found the entry passage six years ago and have used it as a dead drop and cold storage ever since.

The party has reason to believe stolen cargo from the *Uncertainty* — ship's charts, a sealed crate of the Hollowell factor's correspondence, and Delmar's good coat — was routed here after the dry dock break-in.

---

## Access

The entrance is a submerged gap behind the fish market on the harbour-facing wall of the Mercatura, accessible only by water. Characters who can breathe underwater swim through freely. Others must hold their breath for one round (DC 10 CON save to avoid a level of exhaustion if they fail). The gap opens into Room 1, which is above the waterline.

A second entrance exists: a rusted iron grate in the floor of a canal-side warehouse, currently under a Velvet Noose tenant's lease, padlocked (thieves' tools DC 15 or Strength DC 18).

The entire complex sits 3–5 feet below mean canal level. Rooms 1–3 are above the current waterline (damp walls, standing pools). Rooms 4–6 are partially or fully flooded.

---

## General Features

- **Light:** None. The smugglers use hooded lanterns they leave at the entry post (Room 1). Three lanterns remain, one is out of oil.
- **Ceilings:** 8 feet in rooms 1–3. 6 feet in rooms 4–6, lower where the water is deep.
- **Walls:** Pale limestone carved with repeating wave-hands motif — a deity's gesture that is not Umberlee's. The hands are open, palms up. Receiving, not commanding.
- **Sound:** Carries strangely. Voices in Room 3 can be heard as murmurs in Room 5 through a crack in the wall.
- **Smell:** Brine, old wax, and something sweet beneath both — a resinous incense residue that has never fully dissipated from the stone.

## Room 1 — The Drowned Entry

**Dimensions:** 20 × 15 ft. Ceiling 8 ft.

> [!read-aloud]
> Stone steps rise from the water to a landing with an iron post driven into the floor — clearly not original construction. Three hooded lanterns hang from it. The walls on either side bear the same carving, repeated: an open hand, palm up, rendered in pale relief. The hand does not belong to any saint you know. A bolted wooden door stands in the far wall. Voices, maybe, from somewhere past it — or the canal playing tricks through stone.

**Features:**

- The lantern post is a smuggler addition — iron, not carved stone. A peg holds a coil of rope (50 ft.) and a canvas satchel containing flint, tallow candles (6), and a handwritten list in cipher (see **Clues** below).
- The carved hands repeat on both walls, twenty-two total. One hand near the waterline is broken off cleanly — deliberate defacement, old.
- The wooden door is bolted from this side. If the party takes more than 3 rounds to unbolt it, they hear boots on stone from Room 2 (a smuggler who heard the water entry).

**DM note:** This is the orientation room. Let the party notice the pre-Umberlee iconography — open palms, not tridents. Anyone with religion proficiency or Waveservant knowledge (DC 13) recognizes this posture is from an older liturgy and the style predates Umberlee's dominance in this region by at least two centuries.

## Room 2 — The Counting House

**Dimensions:** 30 × 20 ft. Ceiling 8 ft. Two exits: the door to Room 1 and a stone archway leading to Room 3.

> [!read-aloud]
> A long table of salvaged timber sits in the centre of the room, surrounded by mismatched stools. Ledgers, candle stubs, a half-eaten wedge of cheese on wax paper. The walls here have been partially re-plastered — someone tried to cover the old carvings and gave up two-thirds of the way through. A lantern burns low on the table. Two men look up from whatever they were doing and reach for their weapons.

**Encounter — Two Velvet Noose Couriers:**

Both are human. They were not expecting company.

- **Doro Loredan** (uses Bandit stat block, MM p.343) — short, narrow-shouldered, a habitual counter of things. He has the cipher key for the list in Room 1 written on the inside of his left boot.
- **Silla** (uses Thug stat block, MM p.350) — a large woman who does not explain herself. She will fight to buy Doro time to destroy the ledger if she thinks the operation is blown.

**Tactics:** Doro backs toward the ledger immediately on round 1. Silla moves to block the archway to Room 3. Neither will fight to the death — if reduced to 10 HP or below, they disengage and offer to talk. Silla does not know who she works for above her handler; Doro does, and uses that as currency.

**Contents of the Ledger (if recovered):**

- Cargo manifests. The *Uncertainty*'s charts are listed under the notation "H.F. correspondence — sealed — forward Trevisan factor."
- A name: **Batta Zusto** (the broker on the top tier of the Bridge). He is listed as "cleared recipient" for one item — the sealed crate.
- Three other parties' goods, routed through here. Not the party's concern, but potentially leverage.

**Treasure:** Doro carries 14 sp and a folded letter of credit from a Mercatura merchant (20 gp, cashable, not in his name). Silla has 8 sp and a silver ring she will not explain (it belongs to someone she is afraid of — leave it unexplored for now, or use it later).

## Room 3 — The Shrine Nave

**Dimensions:** 40 × 25 ft. Ceiling 10 ft. Three exits: archway from Room 2, stone door to Room 4, and a collapsed section in the north wall (leads to Room 5 via a crawl passage, DC 12 STR or DEX to squeeze through).

> [!read-aloud]
> The plaster ends here. The old stone is bare, and the carvings cover every surface — hundreds of open hands, different sizes, some overlapping, arranged without obvious pattern except density. At the far end, where an altar should be, there is instead a wide stone basin, waist-high, filled with standing water that does not smell like the canal. It smells like rain. In the basin, something catches what little light there is — coins, accumulated over years, dropped in by the smugglers the way sailors touch a good-luck charm. None of them could tell you why they started doing it.

**Features:**

- The basin water is clean (prestidigitation can't explain it — it simply is). Characters who drink from it taste salt water briefly before it resolves to fresh. This is not threatening. It is simply strange.
- If Perrin is in the room, he feels something watching from below the basin — not Umberlee. Not hostile. Just attentive.
- The coins: 3d6 cp, 2d4 sp, and one gold coin of a design no one in the party recognizes (Calven pre-Tessarine, worth 2 gp to a collector, worth much more to the right historian).
- **The Trap (passive):** A pressure stone in front of the basin, original to the shrine. Anyone approaching the basin straight-on triggers a jet of canal water from a concealed pipe in the wall — a purification mechanism, not a weapon. DC 14 Perception (or investigation) to notice before stepping on it. On trigger: the target is soaked and the jet makes a sound audible in Rooms 2 and 4. Not damaging, but loud, and it wets any paper being carried.

**Clues:**

- The crack in the north wall (the crawl passage to Room 5) lets sound through. Characters listening here, with no other noise, can hear breathing from Room 5 — DC 15 Perception.
- The carvings' deity is not named anywhere in the room. The hands are the entire vocabulary. But one archway lintel has a carved inscription in an archaic script: DC 15 History (or Arcana) to partially read: *"...receive what the tide returns..."* The rest is worn to illegibility.

## Room 4 — The Flooded Vestibule

**Dimensions:** 25 × 20 ft. Ceiling 6 ft. Exit to Room 3 (stone door) and an archway to Room 6 (partially underwater). The floor is under 2 feet of water; the water is murky but navigable.

> [!read-aloud]
> The door opens into water. Not much — knee-deep if you step down, ankle-deep at the threshold. The ceiling drops. The far wall has an archway that is half-submerged — beyond it, dark water. Someone has driven iron spikes into the wall at chest height and strung a rope across the room, presumably to haul cargo above the waterline. Three crates hang from the rope on iron hooks.

**The Crates:**

- Crate 1: Wax-sealed, stamped with a merchant mark. Contains 40 lbs of dried fish packed around a false bottom holding 60 gp in loose coin — not the party's. Someone's operational fund.
- Crate 2: The sealed Hollowell factor correspondence. The crate is locked (DC 14 thieves' tools) and stamped with a wax seal already broken by whoever inspected it. Contents: twelve letters between the Hollowell factor in Calveno and a contact in Port Tidefall, discussing the *Uncertainty*'s hull profile in enough detail to suggest Verity Hollowell has been using the factor network to track the ship. This is the [[knighton-pursuit|Knighton Pursuit]] intelligence — significant.
- Crate 3: Delmar's good coat. It has been rifled but not taken — too identifiable. One interior pocket has been opened and re-sewn badly. Whatever was in that pocket is gone. (The DM decides what that was, or leaves it as an open wound.)

**The Hidden Occupant:** A **Giant Crab** (MM p.324) has worked its way in through an underwater gap and has been living in this vestibule for two weeks. It treats the hanging crates as territory. When a character reaches for a crate, it attacks from the water — DC 14 Perception to notice it before the first crate is touched. On failure, it attacks with surprise.

**Flooded Movement:** Difficult terrain. Ranged attack rolls made while standing in the water are at disadvantage (unstable footing). The crab has no such penalty.

## Room 5 — The Warden's Cell

**Access:** Crawl passage from Room 3 (the collapsed wall section). The crawl is 15 feet long and ends in a drop of 2 feet into the room. Anyone entering makes noise (DC 14 Stealth to be quiet).

**Dimensions:** 15 × 15 ft. Ceiling 8 ft. No other exits.

> [!read-aloud]
> The room is small and had one purpose: someone lived here. A stone shelf-bed, a niche in the wall that once held a lamp. A wooden chair that has mostly returned to rot. On the floor, a bedroll that is recent — wool, dry, smelling of a specific person. A canvas pack leans against the wall. Someone is asleep on the bedroll, or was, and they are not here now. The room is not empty.

**The Occupant — Fiola Arman:**

Fiola (she/her) is hiding. She is not a smuggler — she is a chandlery worker from Moretti & Sons who overheard the wrong conversation at the wrong dock and has been in the Reliquary for two days, too frightened to leave, not knowing the party would arrive. She found the crawl passage herself while trying to find a way out that didn't go through the counting house.

**Fiola's stat block:** Commoner (MM p.345). She has a belt knife she will not use effectively.

**What she knows:**

- She saw two men carry cargo through the warehouse grate entrance three days ago. One of them she recognized: **Enzo Segalla**, a canal boat pilot who works the stretch between the Mercatura and the Arsenal. She knows his skiff's name (*Pellegrina*) and his usual route.
- She does not know the smuggling operation's structure, but she heard the name "Zusto" and knows the broker on the Bridge by reputation.
- She has been in this room long enough to notice that the shrine makes sounds. Not threatening sounds. More like the way a building settles — but rhythmic, and sometimes, in the basin room next door, she hears the water move when there is no reason for it to move.

**DM note:** Fiola is a loose end the smugglers don't know about yet. If the party leaves her here, she will eventually be found. If they help her out, she owes them a favour and has a good memory for faces at the dock. She is not a plant. She is simply someone who hid in the wrong place.

**The Pack:** Fiola's belongings. Two days of food (mostly finished), a second pair of socks, a letter from her brother in Dreth she has read so many times the folds are nearly splits, and 4 sp she was going to use for festival tickets. There is also a small clay tile she found on the floor in here — one of the old shrine carvings, broken off and smoothed by water: an open hand, fired in pale clay. She picked it up because it seemed important.

## Room 6 — The Sanctum

**Access:** Through the half-submerged archway from Room 4. Characters must duck underwater for one round to pass through. The far side is above water.

**Dimensions:** 35 × 20 ft. Ceiling 9 ft, angled down toward the back wall. The floor here is dry — raised above the flood level by 18 inches of carved stone plinth. The water that comes in from the archway does not advance past the first 5 feet of floor.

> [!read-aloud]
> You surface into a room that has not been disturbed in longer than the smugglers have been using this place. The floor is dry. The walls are covered floor-to-ceiling in wave-hands — but different from the others: these are full figures, not just hands. Figures standing in water up to their waists, arms raised, heads tilted back. They are not drowning. They are waiting. At the far end of the room, on a raised stone dais, a single object sits beneath a crust of mineral deposit: an altar piece, or what remains of one. The stone has cracked and the object within has been sealed by calcification. You can see the shape of it through the mineral — something roughly spherical, about the size of a fist. The air here is different. Not wrong. Quieter.

**The Sealed Relic:**

The calcified object on the dais contains what was once a ritual focus for the pre-Umberlee deity — a carved sphere of pale stone with a hollow interior containing sea water from a specific tide, sealed with a wax plug centuries ago. The deity it served is genuinely forgotten. There are no records. The Waveservants, if asked, will not confirm or deny its existence and will become uncomfortable.

Removing the calcification requires 10 minutes of work and a hammer and chisel (improvised, DC 12 STR). If freed:

- The sphere is intact. The water inside is still sealed.
- **Identify** reveals it has a faint aura of divination magic — the original ritual intent was prophetic: *"receive what the tide returns."*
- It is not cursed. It is not obviously powerful. But Perrin will feel the same watching presence from this object that he felt in the basin room — the same attention, not Umberlee's, patient in the way that things that have been waiting for centuries are patient.
- Worth 150 gp to a serious collector of pre-Concordat religious objects. Worth something harder to price to the right cleric.

**The Warden's Cache:**

Behind the dais, in a sealed niche sealed with a stone plug (DC 13 Investigation to locate):

- A leather pouch: 22 gp in old Calven coin (pre-Tessarine, collector value double, face value 22 gp).
- A tarnished silver reliquary pendant on a broken chain, engraved with the open-hand motif (25 gp, or more to a historian).
- A wax-sealed scroll, still legible: a tide table for Calveno's harbour in a calendar system no one uses anymore. The mathematical notation is intact, and someone who studies it (DC 18 History, or one week of research) can reverse-engineer the date: the Reliquary was sealed and formally abandoned 214 years ago. The document is not signed but is written in a precise, trained hand.

**DM note:** Nothing attacks in this room. If the party has been noisy throughout the dungeon, let them have this. The room functions as a breath — a genuine mystery that poses no immediate threat and has no tidy answer. Whether the watching presence is significant to Perrin's arc or simply strange texture is a decision for after the session, depending on how the "Grow" entity develops.

## Running This Dungeon

**Pacing:** The dungeon has two encounter rooms (2 and 4), one social room (5), and one mystery room (6). Don't rush Room 6 — if the party reaches it, they've earned the quiet.

**If the party is loud:** Doro and Silla in Room 2 barricade the door before the party arrives. The giant crab in Room 4 is agitated by noise and aggressive from round 1. Fiola in Room 5 is already backed into the corner with her knife out.

**If the party is stealthy:** They can reach Room 3 before encountering anyone. From there they have the initiative on Room 2 (via archway) or Room 5 (via crawl).

**If the party negotiates in Room 2:** Doro will give up the ledger if he believes the operation is already burned. He will not give up Zusto by name without something in return. Silla will not talk about the ring.

**The missing charts:** The party's navigation charts are not here. They were already picked up by Zusto's runner. The ledger lists them as "delivered — 4 days prior." This is correct — finding Zusto is the next step.

**Fiola:** If the party is kind to her and helps her out, note it. She works the Mercatura daily. She has eyes on the dock and a good memory. She is not a named resource yet, but she could become one.
