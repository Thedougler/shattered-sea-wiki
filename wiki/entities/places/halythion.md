# Halythion

## Overview

An ancient sea elf city built into the Teikhinos Reef — invisible from the surface, impassable by ship, older than any colonial record. It measures civilization by what it remembers. Here, memory is law, and law is sung.

**Location:** Eastern Midchain — the Shearwall Reef, approximately 20 miles across

**Depth:** 15–60 feet; city centred on the Koilon

**Theme:** The Aoide — oral law, living memory, civilization as archive

**Patron Deity:** Deep Sashelas

## The City

Forty feet below a reef that looks like nothing, the Koilon opens. Bioluminescence first — amber high, blue-white deep — then arch doorways cut into coral at every tier, then the centre floor, kept clear by centuries of convention. A voice from the floor reaches the rim without loss. This is why the city was built here.

The Teikhinos — the outer reef wall — is not navigable by ship and is not marked on any colonial chart. Surface charts call the reef impassable and route around it; no vessel drawing more than 2 feet can navigate the passage. The route is known only to the sea elves and a small number of authorized outsiders. Halythion has never been found by those who came looking.

The Aoide is not a scripture. It is not stored anywhere. It lives in the mouths of those trained to carry it. The Aoidos — the Singer — is the office responsible for its continuity. The position is formal, hereditary in practice if not in law, and currently incomplete: [[coralyra-dranra|Coralyra]] left her training before the Council declared her ready.

## Fauna

Giant sharks work the outer reef wall, drawn by current upwellings where the deep water hits the reef structure. Large and numerous enough that a longboat crew assessing a grounded vessel has a secondary problem.

Giant octopuses occupy the deeper reef structures — limestone overhangs, wreck debris, and hollows large enough to hold them. The inner reef is known for wrecks. Wrecks accumulate octopus. Divers working the outer salvage edge note that some wrecks appear to have been moved.

Sailors who pass the outer reef margin without grounding and return describe organised shapes moving in formation through the water below the reef break — too large for reef fish, too consistent for current.

## Surface View

The name surfaces in port taverns south of the [[midchain|Midchain]] — usually from someone who heard it from someone else. An underwater city, old, somewhere in the eastern reefs. The reef is impassable by ship and not on any chart worth trusting. Vessels that have gone looking for a passage through have not returned.
