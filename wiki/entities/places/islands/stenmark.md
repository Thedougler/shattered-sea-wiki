# Stenmark

## Overview

Stenmark is the easternmost island in the [[crown-islands|Crown Islands]] — the last Dravosi flag before the Drowned Maw. The name is old: *sten-mark*, stone border. Whoever named it was not being poetic. From the eastern cliffs on a clear day, the water changes colour where the Maw begins — the blue-green of the Scatter going flat and dark, a shade that has no bottom in it. The threshold is visible from shore. People who live on Stenmark have looked at that line their entire lives.

The island is stone and cold water and wind from the east. The [[dravosi-crown|Dravosi Crown]] maintains a signal station on the eastern cliff edge, staffed by a small watch rotation. The people here are fishing people and watch people and the occasional salvager using Stenmark as a staging point for work on the [[shelfworks|Shelfworks]].

## Geography

The island runs north to south, long and narrow. The western face is gentler — settlement, harbour, what farmland the shallow soil supports. The eastern face is sheer cliff from end to end, dropping into deep cold water. The Greyteeth reefs are at their worst in the approaches to Stenmark. Local pilots charge more here than anywhere else in the Crown Islands.

## The Signal Station

Built low against the wind on the eastern cliff edge. The watch rotation is a three-month posting. The station keeps a daily log: Maw surface colouration, fog line position, compass variance, weather anomalies, unusual sightings. Logs go to Harwick Admiralty monthly. Nobody cross-references them systematically with the Sentinels' records — those who have say it would be instructive.

## The Settlement

Stone houses, low profiles, shutters that actually work. The cold upwelling produces deep-water fish species not caught further west — a modest preserved-fish trade with a reputation for flavour. Residents do not explain too carefully what else shares the water with them.

## Fauna

[[high-eyrie|Harpies]] nest in the eastern cliffs — territorial, consistent, present since before the signal station. Giant vultures ride the eastern thermals after storms. Giant sharks work the eastern shelf. The deeper water below the eastern cliff produces wakes, surface disturbances, and shapes the light catches wrong. Stenmark fishermen use a word for these and change the subject.
