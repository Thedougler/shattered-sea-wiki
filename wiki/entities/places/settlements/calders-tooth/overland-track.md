# The Overland Track

## Overview

The Overland Track is the muddy southern switchback road connecting South Harbour to [[port-tidefall|Port Tidefall]] across [[calders-tooth|Calder's Tooth]]. The crossing takes roughly a day and a half on foot or by mount, assuming the weather has not recently made a point.

Wagons break axles daily on corduroy logs laid over the worst mud. The road climbs through wet interior ridge country, then drops again toward the opposite coast, never quite deciding whether it wants to be a road or a tolerated wound in the island.

Locals call it the toll road because every legitimate crossing gives the [[dravosi-crown|Crown]] another chance to count bodies, cargo, and coin.

## Description

The road crosses wet interior ridge country, climbs through switchbacks, runs over corduroy logs through the worst mud, and drops toward the opposite coast. Official enough for tolls and rough enough that carts, mounts, and tired crews all pay for the crossing in more than coin.

## Notable Features

**Southern Switchbacks** — Muddy climbs where pack animals slow and wagons become predictable targets.

**Corduroy Mud Sections** — Log-laid stretches over the worst mud; useful until a wagon breaks an axle on them.

**Drainage Culverts** — Old stone catchment boxes that create warm, dark spider habitat.

## Fauna

The road concentrates the kind of prey a giant axe beak can use — pack animals slow in the mud, wagons stop at switchbacks, and the ridge above gives a running bird room to pace a caravan before dropping onto it. The ridge country is giant axe beak territory.

Giant boars work the wetter ridge sections where the road runs close to interior scrub. Crown track crews flag fresh wallow sign near the road when they find it.

The drainage culverts along the lower switchbacks are giant wolf spider habitat — warm, dark, rarely disturbed. Track maintenance crews carry poles for this reason.

## Hazards

Mud, broken axles, toll delays, switchback choke points, giant axe beak ambushes, boar sounders, and wolf spiders in drainage structures.
