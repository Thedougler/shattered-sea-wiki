# High Line

## Overview

A fishing hamlet built into the northeastern cliffs of [[calders-tooth|Calder's Tooth]], where the cold upwellings come in thick with yellowtail and the rock face drops straight to the water. There is no beach. There are no docks. What there is: massive counterweighted wooden winches bolted to the cliff edge, lowering nets down the face and hauling the catch back up.

The settlement is vertical — ladders, rope platforms, creaking stairways cut into stone, winch-baskets that double as the only transport between levels. The fisherfolk here know every handhold and wind angle. Visitors take longer to learn which gaps are fatal.

## Description

The settlement is vertical — ladders, rope platforms, creaking stairways cut into stone, winch-baskets that double as transport between levels. The fisherfolk here know every handhold and wind angle.

**Winch Line** — Massive counterweighted wooden winches bolted to the cliff edge lower nets down the face and haul the catch back up.

**Rope Platforms** — Work stages, ladders, creaking stairs cut into stone, and winch-baskets that double as transport between levels.

## Fauna

The cold upwelling that makes High Line's nets worth the effort draws hunter sharks into the water column below the cliffs. Winch operators have learned to read the surface for shark sign before sending repair crews down the net face on rope stages. Giant crabs occupy the boulder field at the base of the cliff and can be harvested when surf is low, but the crabs are large enough that the harvesting crew goes armed.

The northern cliff faces carry harpies — not every stretch, but close enough to the upper platforms that the hamlet posts watches during low-wind conditions, when the birds are most active and the winch operation is noisiest.

## Hazards

Hunter sharks below the cliff face, giant crabs at the base, harpies on the northern faces, and vertical drops throughout.
