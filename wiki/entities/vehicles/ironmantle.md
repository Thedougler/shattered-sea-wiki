# Ironmantle

A Valcoran-built remnant sloop, single-masted, its hull plated with iron below the waterline. The iron sits visibly heavy in the water, dark and surface-corroded where it emerges at the waterline, running into cleaner metal below. The timber hull above is old. The join between iron and wood is uneven in a way that no current shipwright would produce. Four iron-tipped sweep oars rack along the inner hull. In choppy water, the hull makes a low resonance that carries up through the deck planking.

## Current State

**Hull:** Worn. Iron plating below waterline intact but shows significant surface corrosion. Timber frame beneath is sound. Repairs to the iron require Valcoran methods no current carpenter knows — standard repairs apply to the timber; iron sections are treated with pitch and rust inhibitor (included in upkeep, effective but not permanent).

**Below Decks:** Small stern cabin, iron-braced walls. Lockable. Fits one occupant. Five hammock hooks forward in a low-ceilinged space. The iron ribbing makes below-decks cramped and loud underway. Minimal hold — iron framing eats cargo space significantly. No galley.

**Identifying features:** Iron plating visible when she heels. Immediately recognisable to anyone who knows Valcoran construction. Distinctive low resonance in choppy water.

## Strengths and Weaknesses

**Strengths:**

- Highest HP and AC of any Tier 1 vessel. Absorbs punishment that would sink a standard sloop.
- Gun mounts more stable in rough weather — no accuracy penalty.
- The iron construction is a psychological deterrent.

**Weaknesses:**

- Speed. Slowest Tier 1 in standard conditions.
- Maneuverability. Does not respond quickly.
- Maintenance. 40 gp/week upkeep is nearly double a standard sloop.
- Structural modification requires ironworking tools.

## Bastion Notes

**Available space:** 4 units. Nothing installed. Iron bracing throughout makes structural modifications difficult. A Carpenter's Shop (1 unit) can be installed in the hold space without ironwork. All other facilities requiring dedicated rooms need a specialist.
