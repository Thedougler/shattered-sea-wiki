# Minotaur

Medium humanoids built heavy — bovine heads, thick curving horns, broad shoulders, hooved feet. A minotaur who has walked a passage once can walk it blindfolded twenty years later. The same faculty works on currents, channel marks, and reef lines.

In the Shattered Sea, minotaurs are navigators. Not exclusively — some farm, some fight, some run chandleries — but the species' cultural gravity pulls toward passage-work: piloting ships through blind channels, mapping reef systems, maintaining the mental charts that keep vessels off the rocks. A minotaur pilot is the most expensive crew hire in the [[midchain|Midchain]] and worth every coin. [[sienne-orre|Sienne Orre]], captain of the [[fernen|*Fernen*]] in [[fisks-fleet|Fisk's Fleet]], ran the fleet's perimeter survey operations — her passage-sense made her the natural choice.

Minotaur populations cluster wherever the water is difficult. Narrow straits, tidal channels, reef knots — the passages that kill ships are the passages minotaurs settle near and learn young. Smaller communities exist in every major port. A minotaur neighborhood is easy to identify from the architecture: the hallways turn.

## Table Traits

- **Size:** Medium (upper end — minotaurs are broad and heavy)
- **Speed:** 30 ft.
- **Horns:** Natural melee weapon; 1d6 piercing on a hit.
- **Goring Rush:** After moving 20+ ft. straight toward a target, bonus action Horn attack. On hit, target makes a Strength save or is pushed 10 ft.
- **Hammering Horns:** After hitting with a melee attack on your turn, bonus action to shove the target with horns. Strength save or pushed 10 ft. and knocked prone.
- **Labyrinthine Recall:** Perfect recall of any path traveled. Advantage on checks to navigate or avoid becoming lost.

## Culture

Minotaur society organizes around **passages** — the word covers physical routes, life stages, and the transitions between them. A young minotaur's first solo navigation of a difficult channel is their passage-rite; the channel becomes part of how close friends refer to them.

Minotaur interiors are complex by design. Switchback hallways, rooms accessible from three sides, staircases that double back. A straight corridor feels to a minotaur the way a windowless room feels to a human — not wrong, exactly, but exposed. Guests are not expected to find the kitchen. The host guides them. This is hospitality, not a test.

**At sea.** A minotaur pilot commands absolute authority over helm and heading in narrow water. A captain who overrides the pilot in a channel is a captain who wants to sink. This convention holds across the Shattered Sea regardless of a ship's flag — even Crown naval officers defer to the pilot in a reef passage.

**Temperament.** Minotaurs run warm and slow. Anger builds visibly — the breathing changes, the head drops, the horns come forward — and a minotaur at that point is extraordinarily dangerous. Most never reach it. The ones who do remember the occasion for the rest of their lives. The stereotype of the raging bull is built from the exceptions, not the rule. A working minotaur pilot radiates the same focused calm as a surgeon mid-operation.

## Naming

Minotaur personal names draw from Greek and Minoan roots: strong consonants, open vowels, -os/-as/-is endings. *Stavra, Kosmos, Thekla, Andronikos.* Sea-going minotaurs in mixed crews often adopt local naming conventions — a minotaur in Calveno waters might carry a name indistinguishable from a Tessarine human. The passage-name tradition persists informally among close kin.
