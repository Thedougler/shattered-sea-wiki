# The Ship

*Fires after [[session-02-scene-03-grigori|Scene 03 — Grigori]] settles. The party has a Crown patrol cutter, a small crew, and no captain. Someone is going to want to look around.*

Full deck-by-deck layout, dimensions, and tactical notes: **[[hcs-surety-layout|HCS Surety Layout]]**. This scene covers what's worth slowing down for.

**[[geoffrey-draves|Geoffrey Draves]]** is the right guide for this. He was the ship's carpenter — he knows every creaking plank, where the copper is lifting, which strakes need recaulking. He also has opinions, delivered quietly, about what he finds. He's committed to the party but he worked on this ship and some of that is still in him.

---

## Ship Roles

Geoffrey does not launch straight into a tour. He stops them at the wheel first, with the manner of someone who has been thinking about this since the fight ended.

*"Before I show you around — you should know what this ship needs to run."*

He ticks it off. Not lecturing. Just practical.

**The Surety runs on ten. Minimum four.** Those four cover:

- Someone at the wheel who makes decisions when things go wrong — **Captain**
- Someone who can read a chart and plot a course without putting them on a reef — **Navigator**
- Someone managing the rigging and the deck crew — **Bosun**
- Someone who knows which planks need caulking before they become a flooding problem — **Carpenter** (he does not gesture at himself, but the pause is its own gesture)

Beyond minimum:

- A **Gunner** to maintain the 6-pounders and not misfire them into the hull
- A **Cook** for any voyage over three days — *"I understand our guest below is useful in a galley,"* he says, without audible irony
- A **Surgeon** — the cabinet is stocked, but Alys has been handling things on her own
- **Marines** for boarding actions, armed watch, anything requiring a blade and a reason to hold ground

Then ordinary sailors. Enough bodies to work the lines and keep the watches. *"You need eight to ten to run her properly. You have five of the old crew and me — if everyone stays."*

He says *if everyone stays* without emphasis. It is still emphasis.

> [!DM]
> [[thunk|Thunk]], the ship's gunner, is present during this conversation and has not been introduced. Geoffrey knows he exists. He did not think to mention it. If a PC looks around and asks who handles the guns, Geoffrey says *"Thunk does"* as if this is obvious and waits for the next question. Thunk waves. He is approximately six foot five and appears very pleased to be acknowledged.
>
> The Captain slot is the scene's quiet center. None of the party has been named captain. [[beaumont-sel|Beaumont Sel]] is the obvious candidate by competence — a Tier 2 former privateer captain who outclasses everyone in this conversation — but Beaumont has his own ship. He'll answer questions freely, give honest assessments of the surviving crew if asked. But when the party sets course, he crosses back to the [[saltwright|Saltwright]] and continues his own route. The party has to put someone at this wheel themselves.
>
> If nobody steps forward, Geoffrey eventually says, flat: *"Someone has to steer."* He looks at each PC in turn. It is more pressure than he intends.
>
> Full role rules and wages: [[ship-stats|Ship Stats]].

## Weather Deck

The party has already spent a session fighting here. Two things worth a beat:

**The lanterns.** Rook kicked them on in Round 1 to collapse Jean-Claude's Umbral Sight. Still burning. Someone has to put them out, or they burn through the night.

**The guns.** Two 6-pounders, bow and stern swivels. All operational. Geoffrey, if asked: *"Crown-issue. Both functional. Powder's below. I'd recommend not firing them without the charge tables — first round might be educational."* He says it like a man who has watched someone skip the charge tables.

**The net trap**, if it was never triggered, is still rigged to the port rail. Geoffrey points it out unprompted: *"That was set for boarding defense. He rigged it himself, the day before we ran into you."* He does not say who the target was. He does not have to.

## Gun Deck

The ship's interior. Where the crew lived.

**Boarding locker** — 8 cutlasses, 4 boarding axes, 6 grappling hooks, 4 loaded flintlocks in a case, rope, 3 smoke signals. Geoffrey: *"Don't ask me why there are more weapons than crew."* The party can arm themselves properly here.

> [!DM]
> **The Bastion.** The Surety is already a functional Tier 1 bastion. The Boarding Locker is the party's **Weapons Locker** — it doesn't need construction, just formal designation. Geoffrey doesn't use that terminology; he just knows where everything is stocked and in what condition. The Chart Table (next room) is the **Chart Archive** — same principle. Two facilities installed, two slots open: Crew Quarters (natural fit: **Crew Berths**) and Galley (natural fit: **Provisions Store**). The medic's cabinet is pre-positioned as a **Surgeon's Berth** the moment someone with the right training fills the Surgeon role. Let this emerge through the tour rather than as a rules announcement — the party should feel like they're discovering what they already have. Full slot mapping: [[hcs-surety-layout|HCS Surety Layout]]. Facility mechanics: [[ship-bastion|Ship Bastion]].

**Chart table** — Crown Islands survey charts covering the eastern waypoints and Central Strait approaches. A compass, dividers, and a 3-month captain's log bolted to the surface. The log is methodical, professional, and increasingly terse. [[beaumont-sel|Beaumont Sel]] appears in three entries — the first is two paragraphs, the second is one, the third is four words: *"Sel. Again. Noted."*

> [!DM]
> The log also has the inspection manifests from the last three months. Anyone reading carefully will notice a pattern: ships with Fisk's Fleet registry get flagged more often than average. It's not written down as policy. It's just the numbers.

**Medic's cabinet** — 2 healer's kits, 1 potion of healing, 1 potion of greater healing (2d4+2), surgical instruments in a sealed roll. The surgeon's berth is pre-built but needs a surgeon. Alys Kuiper has been handling things. She will continue to handle things, with declining enthusiasm, if no one hires a proper surgeon.

**Officer's berth** — two built-in bunks, a shared desk, two locked footlockers (DC 12 Thieves' Tools). Inside: 15 gp total, one spare Crown uniform, an unsigned letter that starts *"I told you this run was wrong"* and then stops.

**Captain's cabin** — Rook watched his crew die from the stern windows in here. The room still has the feel of someone who took the job seriously.

- *Weapons rack:* spare cutlass, one loaded blunderbuss (4d6, 15-ft cone, one shot). Geoffrey: *"I didn't know about the blunderbuss."* He sounds like he means it.
- *Locked chest* (DC 15 Thieves' Tools, or Rook's key): 45 gp, Crown commission papers, sealed letter from the Tidefall Harbormaster regarding [[beaumont-sel|Beaumont Sel]]'s manifest, 1 potion of greater healing (2d4+2), 1 [[potion-of-water-breathing|potion of water breathing]].

> [!DM]
> The letter is sealed with Crown wax. Opening it is a decision — it's addressed to Rook, and breaking it is one of those small choices that tells you what kind of people these are. Contents: the Harbormaster flagging Beaumont's last three manifests as irregularly registered and asking Rook to detain him pending review. Rook apparently decided not to, chose to do a boarding inspection instead, and that is why everyone is here. The irony is available to anyone who reads it.

## Hold

Low overhead (5'8"), six inches of bilge water at the lowest point. Bring a light.

**Inspection storage** — six months of boarding manifests, a fee ledger, and three blank Crown writs in a sealed folder. The writs are forgeable Crown authority. Ensign Wouters, if he is with the party, looks at the blank writs and then very deliberately looks at something else.

**Seized crates (×2, unlabeled)** — The fee ledger in inspection storage lists them as "confiscated goods, pending Crown review." They have never been opened for Crown review, because Rook never intended to send them for Crown review. The padlocks are simple (DC 10 Thieves' Tools or a crowbar).

*Crate 1:* Two silver candelabras (25 gp each), a locked jewelry box (DC 12; inside — a small pendant worth 15 gp, a name engraved on the back: *"For Mira, from the sea"*), a cask of fine brandy (12 gp, ostensibly seized for unpaid duties), a bolt of Calveno silk (20 gp). None of this is formally receipted as Crown property. It is simply here. **Subtotal: ~97 gp.**

*Crate 2:* Two flintlock pistols in good condition (loaded, 15 gp each), [[miras-blade|Mira's Blade]] — a +1 shortsword with a distinctive notched pommel, the kind of blade that belongs to a specific person rather than a ship's locker — and a sealed package labeled in Rook's hand: *"Contraband — R.B."* Inside: twenty vials of Grung poison tincture, sealed in wax, worth ~5 gp each to the right buyer (100 gp total as black-market contraband). Geoffrey stares at these for a moment and says nothing.

> [!DM]
> Anyone who cross-references the fee ledger in Inspection Storage against the crate contents will find the discrepancy immediately: the official manifest records a fraction of what's actually here. The rest was lifted. This is how Rook supplemented his income.
>
> Jean-Claude will recognize the tincture vials on sight — the preparation method is the same one the Alchemist used on deck. They are enough to kill a roomful of people or fund a significant purchase; the party decides what to do with them. The pendant in Crate 1 and Mira's Blade share an origin — the blade was taken from the same inspection that yielded the jewelry box. Whether "Mira" is the sword's owner, the pendant's recipient, or the same person is deliberately unresolved. The DM should decide when that thread becomes relevant, not now.

**Locked cargo crate** (DC 14 Thieves' Tools) — 150 gp in mixed coin and a folded map. The map shows an anchorage marked *"Galewall"* with a bearing and a rough depth sounding. No name. No context. The sounding suggests shallow water over something large.

> [!DM]
> Whether the party found this crate in play is unconfirmed per the session-02 canonical summary. Do not mark these contents found without a second pass.

**Hidden stash** (DC 13 Investigation in the captain's cabin — a loose panel under the bunk, detectable by the lack of dust in the seam) — 110 gp in small-denomination coin packed in oilcloth, two unset garnets (20 gp each). This is Rook's real personal savings, the money he didn't want even the purser's ledger to know about.

**Prisoner brig** — iron cage bolted to the keel. And hanging from a bent ship nail in the keel timber, a brass birdcage roughly two feet across. The moment someone enters with a light, something inside it moves — fast, toward the bars, proboscis first.

[[ket|Ket]]. A [[moucheron|Moucheron]], taken from [[murrat|Murrat]] by Rook three weeks ago as a specimen. Unarmed, untrained, and actively hostile. The crew fed him on rotation — a donor would approach, arm through the bars, and had to pull back fast or he'd latch. Nobody has been down since the Saltwright engagement started.

*"You're bleeding,"* he says, if anyone in the party took damage and hasn't been fully healed. He says it the way you'd note the weather.

> [!DM]
> Full NPC: [[ket|Ket]]. Currently **Difficult** on the hunger track — 36+ hours without a feed. He will attempt to feed the moment anything gets within proboscis range of the bars. He uses Common to invite people closer. He is not being diplomatic; he is being a predator.
>
> The cage latch is DC 8 Sleight of Hand from outside. He cannot open it from inside.
>
> **If opened:** He feeds on whoever is closest and within reach, then flies for open air — out a gun port, through the hatch, gone. He is not making a speech about it. He wants blood and sky, in that order.
>
> Geoffrey stops at the brig doorway and does not come in. *"Rook brought that back from Murrat. The crew's been feeding it. I don't know why."* He sounds like a man who has been asking himself that question for three weeks.
>
> If anyone gets within 5 feet of the cage: DC 12 Dexterity save or the proboscis finds a gap in the bars. On a hit: 1d6 piercing damage and Ket is Satiated until his next scene. He grins about this. It is the worst grin.

> **Party treasury note:** The Surety has immediate running costs. Before individual shares are divided, the party should consider pulling ~120 gp off the top into a ship's fund. Rough expenses ahead:
> - **Crew wages** — 2 sp/day per sailor; ~6 crew (if everyone stays) = ~36 gp/month. Geoffrey works for passage/shares, not wages, but the others expect pay.
> - **Repainting** (to lose the Crown registry) — ~75 gp at a working yard like Kalowe; ~150 gp at Tidefall (better work, higher fees, more questions).
> - **Port fees, provisions, incidentals** — 5–15 gp per port depending on size.
>

**Powder magazine** — 12 cannon charges (sealed copper canisters), 20 pistol charges. Geoffrey stops at the threshold and says, without preamble: *"No open flames. Not ever. Not for any reason. That's not a suggestion."* He waits until everyone nods.

## The Crew

By the time the hold tour finishes, the old Surety crew has had an hour to take stock of their situation. They're not hiding. They're not hostile. They're waiting to see what kind of management this is.

Each of them defaults to leaving at the next port. Not out of anger. It's simply what sailors do when a ship changes hands under fraught circumstances. The party can change this, one conversation at a time.

---

**[[sem-holst|Sem Holst]]** is already cataloguing damage Geoffrey didn't mention. He'll tell you exactly what's wrong with this hull and exactly how long it will take to fix, and he will not soften either number. He wants to know if the party is actually serious about maintaining the vessel — not what it can do, but the ship itself. If a PC demonstrates they know the difference between a caulked seam and a packed one, or asks a question about the hull that isn't about how fast it can go, he reconsiders. *"Not many privateers bother learning that."* He says it the way you'd admit something surprised you.

---

**Ensign Wouters** was at the chart table before anyone asked him to be — per prep notes. In play, Wouters was killed during the boarding. The Navigator post is open.

---

**[[alys-kuiper|Alys Kuiper]]** is in the medic's cabinet doing inventory. She finds this calming. She will announce, without particular inflection, that healer's kit supply is adequate for two more field engagements and that the surgical instruments are well-maintained except for one probe that needs replacing. She is not asking to stay. She is not asking to leave. She is performing the inventory because someone has to and she is the most qualified, and she would like that fact to be acknowledged. If the party formally names the post — Surgeon, ship's medical officer, something that constitutes an actual role — she stays. If they mention they intend to keep the Surgeon's Berth staffed and stocked, she looks at the cabinet the way Holst looked at the hull: assessing what it could be.

---

**[[old-faas|Old Faas]]** is up in the rigging. He went up right after Grigori gave the nod and he hasn't come down. He is not hiding. He just prefers to be useful in ways that keep him off the deck and out of conversations. He'll come down when needed. The argument to stay is simple: ask for his assessment of the rigging, and listen to the full answer. He will give a detailed, quietly passionate, twelve-minute account of every line, block, and splice and what each needs. Anyone who doesn't walk away partway through gets an evaluating look. *"Most people leave before the fore-topgallant."*

---

**[[noor|Noor]]** is near the galley, failing to be invisible due to enthusiasm. They have a sailing manual open and are reading it while standing up. They are the easiest to recruit — they haven't decided where they're going and they want, more than anything, to be on a ship that goes somewhere interesting. Any destination the party names that isn't immediately familiar prompts them to check the manual. If it's not in the manual, they look thoughtful. This is apparently a point in the destination's favor.

---

**[[thunk|Thunk]]** came through the locked door last, unhurried, and has since drifted toward the gun deck with the easy gravity of a man returning to something he was interrupted from. He is the Surety's gunner. He does not mention this. If the party skips the hold tour, they may reach their first destination never knowing he was there.

He is enormous. The Surety's interior doorframes did not anticipate him. He smells of old iron and powder and something faintly sweet, which turns out to be the tallow he packs around the shot pouches. He left blacksmithing because the sea called him, and when pressed on what that means he says *"very loud, actually — I cannot sleep sometimes, so I come here instead"* with complete sincerity.

He will answer any question with full, earnest detail. Ask about the guns. Ask about the charge tables. Ask about his hands. He has good answers to all of these and he would genuinely like to share them, in the direct, slightly formal cadence of someone translating from a language he learned before Common. The warmth is real. The enthusiasm for things that explode is also real.

His condition to stay is simply that someone asked. Not a formal role. Not a title. One conversation where the other person wanted the answer.

> [!DM]
> **Thunk is the best hire in this scene and he will not tell them that.**
>
> Mechanically: when Thunk mans a cannon, he counts as two crew for the purpose of determining how many guns can be manned. If the party ever installs a **Carpenter's Shop** (Smithy facility) on the Surety, he can run it without additional staff. He left blacksmithing for the sea but the forge is still in him.
>
> The accent is Arnold Schwarzenegger. Not a joke — play it straight, with the short declarative sentences and the earnest odd emphases. Sample lines: *"Ja, I like this gun. She has good — temperament."* / *"The charge ratio, I already calculate. You want to hear?"* / *"Do not worry. I am very careful. Mostly."* / *"I was a blacksmith. But the sea, she is louder."*
>
> The roleplay reward is inverted: he does not need to be persuaded or noticed or given a reason to feel useful. He just needs someone to ask the right question. The right question is anything about the guns, the powder, or the work.

---

> [!DM]
> These conversations don't need to be formal scenes. They happen naturally as the party moves through the ship. Reward any PC who engages: Perception to notice what Holst is cataloguing, Insight to read what Alys is actually asking for, Investigation to give Wouters something useful to work with at the chart table. None of these are hard sells — they just need a reason, and the reason can be small.
>
> Track who commits before the party sets course. All five are functional crew. Losing any of them creates a real gap in the role table established at the start of this scene.

## The Ship, as a Thing

After the tour, there's a moment where it settles. This is their ship. It needs a crew of ten to run properly and they have five survivors, one carpenter, and whatever the party brings. It has two functioning cannons and a registry plate that says it belongs to the Dravosi Crown. It has a chart table with the Strait fully mapped. It has 6 tons of usable cargo space and a boarding locker stocked for a crew that no longer exists.

Geoffrey, if no one says anything: *"She's sound. Needs work, but she's sound. I know where the problems are."* He pauses. *"What do you want to do with her?"*

That's the question. Move to [[session-02-scene-05-central-strait|Scene 05 — Central Strait]] once the party sets a course.
