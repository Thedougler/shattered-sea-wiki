# The Draves Bloodline Question

The Draves family name appears in Crown genealogical archives in sections that predate the current colonial administration by more generations than a family of their standing would normally explain. [[aldric-drave|Aldric Drave]] built the Crown's genealogical apparatus to track his bloodline. The Draves entry is in those records.

Whether [[geoffrey-draves|Geoffrey]] is a functioning phylactery — whether his bloodline carries enough of the original potency to interest Aldric's heir-selection process — is deliberately unresolved. The surname is one letter off from Drave. The archival depth is anomalous. Neither fact requires the other to be true.

> [!dm]
> Leave this open. Do not commit either way.

## Key Actors

- [[geoffrey-draves|Geoffrey Draves]] — the crew member with the anomalous surname; does not know about the genealogical record; has other concerns
- [[aldous-draves|Aldous Draves]] — Geoffrey's father; has access to Crown archives through factor work; noticed the depth of the Draves record and filed it under "old family"; does not know what the depth means
- [[aldric-drave|Aldric Drave]] — built the genealogical apparatus; would be interested in a Draves-branch descendant disrupting Crown infrastructure

## The Aldous Angle

Aldous's factor work gives him routine access to Crown administrative archives. He has noticed the depth of the Draves genealogical record. He does not question it — the administrative culture treats deep documentation as legitimacy, not a red flag. He does not know what it means.

## How It Surfaces

If the party searches deep Tidefall genealogical records, the Draves entry is there and it goes back further than expected. If Aldric Drave becomes aware that a Draves-branch descendant is disrupting Crown infrastructure alongside a foreign privateer crew, his interest is at least plausible.

Pull this thread when the campaign needs it. Let it lie until then.

## Geoffrey at the Table

**Low pressure:** He is the crew member who can assess the ship honestly. Give him small moments where his carpentry opinion matters — hull before a crossing, a repair that needs doing, a choice between speed and safety. He earns trust this way without being pushed.

**Medium pressure:** The dancing surfaces. Someone sees him. The party knows something he has not told them — a moment where Geoffrey decides whether to be embarrassed, defiant, or honest.

**High pressure:** The party needs something in Tidefall, Knighton becomes relevant, the Aldric bloodline thread surfaces, Verity's ledger problem becomes a party problem, or Geoffrey learns the full wage picture.

**The vow test:** The party asks him to do something that costs him something toward his goal. He said any means. The campaign asks him to find out what that actually means in practice.

**Uncle Knighton:** Do not escalate until [[dorian-bishop|Dorian Bishop]] is resolved. Blood connection may generate a hearing from Knighton; it will not generate protection. Whether a fleet admiral would extend professional consideration to a nephew who defected from a Crown vessel is a question best left until there is something concrete riding on the answer.

## Pressures

- Aldric's genealogical apparatus already contains the Draves entry — the interest is structural, not contingent on player action
- Geoffrey's dancing is surfacing: less careful than he was on the Surety; someone on this crew is going to see him

## If Ignored

The bloodline question stays dormant. Geoffrey's dancing surfaces on its own timeline regardless. Aldric takes no action unless a Draves-branch descendant enters his active threat perimeter.
