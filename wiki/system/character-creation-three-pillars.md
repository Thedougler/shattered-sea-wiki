# Character Creation: Three Pillars

> Agent use: Read before helping create or evaluate a player character concept.

## Fast Rules

- Start with gimmick, conflict, and want before mechanics.
- Conflict must be personal, actionable, and campaign-facing.
- Want gives the character forward motion; backstory explains why.
- Use the three pillars to find hooks, not to lock an arc.

## Pillar I — Gimmick

The most recognizable and most shallow aspect of the character. Creates an instant, legible impression at the table.

The Gimmick is a clear point of view — something another player can grasp immediately.

Examples: Quick to Anger, Scaredy-Cat, Jokester, Absolute Idiot, Know-it-All, Excessively Naive, Prissy Noble, Morbid and Creepy, Compulsive Liar.

## Pillar II — Conflict

The main issue the character is currently facing. Must be personal, not vague. Provides direction for growth and drives backstory toward arcs that can develop through play.

Examples: Persecuted for a crime they didn't commit; evading authorities; disowned by family; losing a loved one; subject of a prophecy; pursuing an unsolved mystery.

## Pillar III — Want

The character's deepest desire at story start. A character with a Want has agency — they seek things rather than having the story happen to them. The Want can evolve into a **Need** through play: related to but distinct from the original Want, shaped by what the character has experienced.

Examples: Find out who my parents are; be accepted by family; become strong enough to need no one; make those who wronged me suffer; become rich; bring a loved one back from the dead.

## How the Pillars Interact

- **Gimmick** — the surface. What other players see first.
- **Conflict** — the wound or external pressure. What the character is up against.
- **Want** — the engine. What the character is reaching toward.

The Want defines the goal, the Conflict creates the obstacle, the Gimmick is the behavior pattern the character must grow beyond (or lean into) to resolve both.
