# Dungeon Creation Method

> Agent use: Read before generating a dungeon, ruin, lair, or enclosed adventure site.

## Fast Rules

- Pick one theme; make every mechanic and room express it.
- Define the dungeon goal before mapping encounters.
- Encounters should test choices, not just drain resources.
- Endings should reveal consequence, cost, or transformation.

## The Method

**Step I — Theme.** Define the theme in **one word** to avoid convolution. A theme sets the mood and determines setting. Everything else derives from it.

> Examples: *lies* → a carnival of deception; *healing* → an abandoned hospital; *death* → a noble family mausoleum.

**Step II — Mechanics.** Select game mechanics that express the theme — rules you can tweak, use, or introduce to make players *feel* the theme through gameplay, not just flavour.

> Examples: *lies* → illusion magic, Insight/Perception checks; *healing* → HP regen, hit dice, healing magic; *death* → necromancy, death saves, undead.

**Step III — Goal.** Define what success looks like — what players must achieve to complete the dungeon. Vary goals across a campaign to maintain engagement.

> Examples: *lies* → rescue an NPC trapped under enchantment; *healing* → recover a soul-healing potion recipe; *death* → defeat the undead patriarch commanding his reanimated kin.

**Step IV — Encounters.** Three types:

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Combat** | Fighting encounters using theme mechanics |
| **Puzzle / Trap** | Environmental challenges expressing the theme |
| **Roleplay** | Social or narrative encounters tied to theme |

Vary encounter types for pacing and to explore the theme from multiple angles.

**Step V — Ending.** The climax. Synthesize all prior steps into a final encounter that uses every mechanic and aspect of the theme introduced through the dungeon.

> Examples: *lies* → players must lie to the enchanted NPC to lead them out; *healing* → defeat a creature by healing it rather than damaging it; *death* → only a PC rolling death saves can slay the patriarch in the realm of the dead.

## Design Principles

- Theme drives every decision — setting, mechanics, encounters, ending must all reinforce it.
- Express theme mechanically, not just descriptively: players should *interact* with the theme.
- A single-word theme prevents scope creep.
- The ending is a culmination: bring together all mechanics and theme expressions from the dungeon.
