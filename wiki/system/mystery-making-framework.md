# Mystery-Making Framework

> Agent use: Read before creating clues, investigations, culprit logic, or mystery arcs.

## Fast Rules

- Know the mystery, culprit, motive, and method before placing clues.
- Every required conclusion needs multiple clues.
- Clues should point to action, not just information.
- Simplify the method before adding more evidence.

## The Three Core Questions

Answer in order before creating any clues. All clue design depends on solid answers to all three.

**What is the Mystery?** Define the central unknown the party must resolve. Murder, theft, a series of events — any scenario where the answer is not clear from the start and must be found through investigation.

**Who Did It?** Identify the culprit. Can be one person, multiple accomplices, a non-human agent, or no culprit at all. Simple mysteries start with a single perpetrator; complex ones can branch.

**Why Did They Do It?** The motive. Sets the tone — a goofier motive suits a comedic adventure; a sympathetic or emotionally complex motive suits a grittier one. Motive defines how players feel about the culprit when revealed.

**How Did They Do It?** The method. This is the primary difficulty dial — more moving parts means a harder mystery. If the method is too complex or esoteric, clue generation becomes impossible; simplify the method before proceeding.

## Clue Rules

- Each clue must tie back to at least one of the three questions (Who / Why / How).
- Each question must have **at least 3 clues** pointing toward its answer.
- More complicated method = more clues must explicitly address it.
- Clues can be physical objects or NPC conversations unlocked by asking the right questions.
- Assign every clue to a specific location, NPC, or event — gate behind combat, puzzles, or traps as appropriate.
- Stay flexible: if a player looks somewhere reasonable that wasn't the planned location, move the clue there and reward the deduction.
- If you can't generate 3 clues for any part of the method without forcing specific NPC dialogue, the method is too complex — revise it.

## Design Notes

- The 3-clues-per-question rule prevents players from getting stuck without GM hints (which undercut player satisfaction).
- Mysteries break up combat-heavy stretches by shifting focus to deduction, roleplay, and logic.
- Tailor to your group — some prefer deduction-heavy play, others want combat integrated throughout.
