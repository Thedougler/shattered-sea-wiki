# One-Shot Modular Method (MMOS)

> Agent use: Read before designing a one-shot or session that must survive time cuts.

## Fast Rules

- Pre-communicate premise and goal before play.
- Build removable encounters so time cuts do not break the session.
- Use two paths, one key, modular encounters, showdown, and ending.
- Keep the hook concrete enough to start immediately.

## Step 0: Above Table (Pre-Game)

Do these before play begins:
1. Tell players the quest in advance.
2. Have players describe their characters to each other (skip in-game introductions).
3. Communicate that the goal is to finish in one session.

## Step 1: The Inciting Quest

The one-shot begins after the players have accepted the quest. The opening scene can be immediately post-briefing, at the quest location, or en route.

> Examples: Find the Amulet, Defeat the Demon, Win the Race, Escort the Duke, Survive the Night, Unmask the Killer, Escape the Labyrinth.

## Step 2: The Two Paths

| Path | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Path of Least Resistance** | Most obvious route — goes directly to completing the quest. |
| **Path Least Traveled On** | Meandering route — extra encounters, ends with finding the Key. |

## Step 3: The Key

A reward for taking the Path Least Traveled On — a literal object or information that recontextualizes the quest and unlocks Ending B.

> Examples: The Amulet is Cursed, the Demon is Nice, the Race is Rigged, the Duke is Dead, There is More than One Killer, the Labyrinth Has Two Levels.

## Step 4: The Encounters

**Most must be modular** — easy to skip without breaking the one-shot. Each encounter either stands alone or includes a bypass that delivers whatever the party needs to continue.

Recommended minimum:

| Type | Count |
|------|-------|
| Combat | 2 |
| Roleplay | 1 |
| Exploration / Puzzle | 1 |

## Step 5: The Showdown

The only non-modular encounter. Cannot be skipped. Usually a boss battle — can be any encounter type. If the Key changes the circumstances, keep the mechanical core the same and change only the flavour.

## Step 5: The Endings

| Ending | Trigger |
|--------|---------|
| **Ending A (Adequate)** | No Key required — quest completed the obvious way. |
| **Ending B (Ballsy)** | Key required — players explored the alternate path; the Key must visibly affect the outcome. |

## Step 6: The Hook

An implied continuation — suggests further adventures ahead. Make hooks depend on player choices during the session.

## Design Principles

- Modularity is the primary structural goal — most encounters must be skippable.
- Player choice must be real: both paths and both endings must meaningfully differ.
- The Key is the mechanism that makes Ending B feel earned.
- Pre-game communication (Step 0) is as important as any in-session design choice.
