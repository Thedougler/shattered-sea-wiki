# Player Agency

> Agent use: Read when a prep or ruling choice might constrain, erase, or reward player choice.

## Fast Rules

- Choices need visible stakes and honest consequences.
- Do not solve player problems with hidden author force.
- Rule of Cool is a reward, not a default override.
- Ask what players want before optimizing around them.

Player agency is the North Star. Everything at the table exists to serve it: the rules, rulings, narrative consequences, and the world itself.

## How Rules Serve Agency

Rules are the shared framework that makes make-believe feel real. Without rules, player choices have no weight because there are no meaningful consequences. Enforcing rules is an act of respect for agency — it gives player decisions actual stakes. A world where the DM bends rules whenever convenient is a world where player choices don't matter.

## Rule of Cool

Rule of Cool sits above RAW as a reward, not a default. Use sparingly: for exceptional roleplay, for creative problem-solving that earns it, and only when it doesn't undermine the rules that give other players' choices meaning. It loses power if it becomes expected.

**Order in practice:** enforce rules as written, bend them occasionally as a reward, let everything else serve the players.

## Shared Ownership

The campaign belongs to everyone at the table. In a party of four, the story is at most 20% the DM's — the DM does 80% of the work. That's the deal. More work does not confer more ownership. If a player calls it "the DM's campaign," correct them immediately: "No, this is our story."

## What Threatens Agency

- Writing stories instead of situations (scripting outcomes instead of letting choices land)
- Over-prepping to the point of quietly writing player choices out
- Banning or nerfing content players have built identities around
- Pulling punches so deaths can't happen (removes stakes)
- Not asking players what they want before the campaign starts
