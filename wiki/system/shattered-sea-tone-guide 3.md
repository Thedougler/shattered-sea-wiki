# Shattered Sea — Tone Guide

> Agent use: Read before generating Shattered Sea NPCs, scenes, dialogue, factions, or descriptions.

## Fast Rules

- Tone equation: high competence trapped inside low emotional maturity.
- Scenes need real danger, petty internal friction, and stylish presentation.
- NPCs should be capable, vain, wounded, and socially sharp.
- Comedy comes from character flaws; the world stays dangerous.

## Core Equation

**High competence trapped inside low emotional maturity.**

Real danger, glamour, hierarchy, and momentum — populated by stylish, capable people who let ego, vanity, lust, boredom, resentment, and old grudges complicate their best efforts.

**One-sentence tone:** Swashbuckling sophistication often complicated by pettiness.

## Six Pillars

**1. Real stakes + childish dysfunction.** The setting plays straight. The people are disasters. Storms, monsters, betrayal, and sorcery are deadly and consequential. The immediate complications are stupidly personal. Pattern: *epic consequences, petty triggers.*

**2. Competence without adulthood.** Characters are dangerous experts and personal disasters. Brilliant captain, terrible impulse control. Feared corsair, desperate for approval. Elegant diplomat, cannot let an insult go.

**3. Stylish surface, rotten interior.** Lacquered warships and gilt figureheads. Velvet officers with enchanted pistols. Perfumed courts built on piracy or exploitation. Fine wine, blood on cuffs, smiling hatred.

**4. Banter as combat.** Dialogue is social violence: interruption, one-upmanship, flirtation as dominance, sarcasm masking hurt, precision insults, status testing, old slights never forgotten. Not random quips — weaponised wit.

**5. Mundane pettiness in lethal situations.** A boarding action pauses to argue about bad gunpowder. A kraken attack becomes a paperwork problem. A treaty fails over seating and décor. A villain summons a leviathan over a personal slight.

**6. Cynicism without emptiness.** Institutions are dysfunctional clown cars run by egomaniacs. Yet people still have messy loyalty, attraction, history, and occasional genuine care. Mock heroism — but never so hard that nothing matters.

## Tone Boundaries

| Keep | Avoid |
|------|-------|
| Glamour, danger, decadence, speed | Broad clown comedy or pure parody |
| Sharp dialogue and recurring grudges | Meme humor or nonstop one-liners |
| Emotional damage played lightly | Making the world itself too silly |
| Stylish people behaving badly | Every NPC sarcastic in the same voice |
| Real consequences | Removing consequences |

## NPC Construction Formula

> **Public image + real competence + one humiliating need + one petty fixation**

- Naval legend / brilliant tactician / needs royal approval / obsessed with seeming younger than rival
- Scandalous aristocrat / master of boarding warfare / fears tenderness / ruins diplomacy if wardrobe is insulted
- Aloof intellectual / terrifying storm-caster / needs recognition / starts feuds over titles and terminology

## Scene Construction Rule

Every major scene combines:

1. A real external threat (battle, storm, monster, curse, betrayal)
2. A petty internal conflict (insult, jealousy, rank dispute, old fling, unpaid tab)
3. Stylish presentation (beauty, wealth, elegance, rot)
4. Fast verbal escalation (interrupting, misunderstanding, dominance, callback)
5. Consequences that remain real (injury, debt, scandal, ship damage, political fallout)

## Agent Application

- Default NPC dialogue register: status-testing, mildly competitive, guarded about real needs.
- Default scene energy: competent professionals sabotaged by interpersonal baggage.
- Default world texture: beautiful, expensive, slightly rotten.
- When generating NPCs, apply the construction formula. A flat, professional NPC is a missed opportunity.
- When generating scenes, layer a petty conflict onto the tactical situation.
- Comedy comes from character flaws, not world silliness. The sea is deadly. The monsters are real. The people are disasters.
