# Situations Not Stories

> Agent use: Read before turning plot ideas into playable pressure without scripted outcomes.

## Fast Rules

- Create factions, goals, pressures, and clocks.
- Do not script the solution or the sequence of scenes.
- Ask what changes if players do nothing.
- Prep movable parts that can collide in play.

Core prep principle: write situations, not stories. Drop the players into something interesting and find out what they do. Whatever they do next is the story.

## The Distinction

**A story** has a beginning, middle, and end. You can only write one of those before the session starts. Every plot you've written becomes a suggestion at best and dead weight at worst once players sit down.

**A situation** has: factions with goals; a problem with no obvious solution; pressure that builds if nobody acts; enough moving parts that almost anything the players do produces an interesting result.

You don't need to know how it ends. You need to know what is happening when they arrive, who wants what, and what happens next if the players do nothing.

## Example

**Story framing:** "The players must stop the cultists from completing the ritual." — has a correct answer, a failure state the DM controls, and a script players are executing.

**Situation framing:** "The cultists are midway through a ritual, the city guard is closing in from outside, a rival faction wants the artifact for themselves, and the players just walked through the door." — no script; multiple actors with independent goals; any player action produces a different result.

## Building a Situation

1. Who is here and what do they want?
2. What happens if the players do nothing for an hour? A day? A week?
3. What are two or three things a party might do that would produce very different outcomes?

If #3 leads to the same place regardless, it's a story beat, not a situation. Redesign it or cut it.
