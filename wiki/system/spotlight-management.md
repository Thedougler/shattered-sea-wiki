# Spotlight Management

> Agent use: Read before session prep when a PC needs meaningful attention or reward design.

## Fast Rules

- Track who has not had a meaningful moment recently.
- Engineer opportunities, not guaranteed victories.
- Use rewards to reinforce what each player enjoys.
- Avoid solving imbalance by silencing engaged players.

Spotlight isn't distributed automatically. The DM must actively watch the table and engineer opportunities for players who are disappearing. Players shouldn't have to fight for space in their own story.

## The Problem

Some players always push forward; others hang back. Neither tendency is wrong. But if the table runs itself, the loudest players fill every scene and the quieter ones gradually disappear — often before it's obvious to anyone.

## The Two-Level Response

**In-session:** Everyone rolls at least once every ten minutes. When a player has gone quiet, ask what they're doing, improvise a relevant skill check, let them roll and narrate the result. The roll signals: you're here, you matter, what happens next is partly yours.

**Long-view:** End-of-session awards tracking shows who has been invisible over time. If a player consistently isn't winning or being nominated, adjust encounters to give them more opportunities.

## Engineering Spotlight

To pull a quiet player in without breaking the scene:

- Give them information nobody else has
- Put an NPC in front of them who addresses them specifically
- Build the next scene around a situation that only makes sense for their character to lead

Don't announce it. Engineer the opportunity and let them take it.

## End-of-Session Awards

Vote at the end of every session. Aim for as many categories as you have players.

| Award | For |
|---|---|
| Combat MVP | Most impactful or creative play in combat |
| Best Roleplay | Best played character strengths and weaknesses |
| Funniest Play | The moment that had the table losing it |
| Most Creative Solution | The play nobody else would have thought of |
| Best Team Player | Setting someone else up to shine, taking the hit |
| Best Character Moment | A scene or decision that perfectly captured who their character is |

Each winner banks an inspiration. Track nominations over time; patterns show who needs engineered opportunities.
